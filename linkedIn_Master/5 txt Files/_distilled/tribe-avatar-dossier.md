# Tribe Avatar Dossier — Uncoded Hub / The Coach Authority System

Distilled from "The Tribe Dossier" (Matt Lakajev, sevenfigurecreators.com), applied to Deepak's audience of coaches and consultants and his 14,500 LinkedIn connections.

## Part 1 — The framework, distilled

The core claim: most people define their audience as a **demographic** ("business owners," "coaches") when they need to define a **tribe** — a group with its own language, gathering places, enemies, entry costs, and values. A demographic is a label. A tribe is a house you can walk through room by room. The dossier scores a tribe on "room count" (0–16); under 9 filled rooms means you probably have a demographic, not a tribe, and content built on it will feel generic.

The dimensions, in order of how much they're weighted:

1. **House Type (identity origin).** Every tribe is one or more of three types: **Chosen** (they paid a cost to join — certification, ordeal, financial buy-in — and feel "I earned my place"), **Circumstantial** (life put them here — a diagnosis, a layoff, a life stage — and they feel "only someone who's been through this understands"), or **Place** (geography or a physical world shaped them). Strongest tribes stack two or three types.

2. **The 5-Word Test and 5-Object Test.** Can you name 5 words that *only* this tribe uses (not industry jargon everyone knows), and 5 objects/tools/brands that only this tribe owns? If words and objects "leak" into adjacent groups, you haven't found the tribe yet — you're one layer too broad.

3. **The Main Rooms (~60% of tribe density) — the load-bearing dimensions:**
   - **Language Room** — jargon, complaints (often more tribal than jargon), and brand debates.
   - **Front Room** — do they know they're a group? Named communities, "we" language, spottable-from-a-profile identity.
   - **The Fence** — a *named, external* shared enemy (not "negativity" — a specific institution, approach, or type of person).
   - **The Foundation** — the cost of entry (formal, experiential, financial, or ordeal). If anyone can claim membership for free, there's no foundation.
   - **The Hearth** — sacred values they won't compromise, and the unwritten rule that instantly outs a fake.

4. **The Drawers (~30% of density) — deeper texture:** identity fusion, status hierarchy, rituals/calendar, collective memory, material culture, shared timestamps, shared suffering, reputation economy, common fate, physical territory, competition.

5. **The Kitchen Counter (keeps it current):** this week's news/debates in their world, the 3–5 named people they collectively follow, what tools/brands are hot or falling, and "a day in this house" — an hour-by-hour walk-through of their actual day, not their job title.

6. **Amplifiers (~10%):** group size, kinship ties, participatory culture, social proof, economic lock-in, mission, parasocial leadership, temporal depth.

The meta-point: content only lands when it's written from inside a specific room you've actually verified — via real client conversations, not guessed from a persona template. Empty rooms aren't failures; they're this week's research/DM targets.

## Part 2 — Applied: Uncoded Hub's avatar

**Tribe (working hypothesis):** Independent coaches and consultants, 1–6 years into building their own practice, who have real expertise and a client base built on referrals and LinkedIn — but whose online presence embarrasses them the moment a serious lead actually looks them up.

**House Type — stacked:**
- *Chosen*: many paid real entry costs — ICF/NLP/certification programs, years of unpaid practice hours, leaving a stable job to "hang out a shingle."
- *Circumstantial*: nearly all arrived via a rupture — burnout, a layoff, a health scare, a corporate ceiling — that pushed them into coaching as a second act. That shared origin story is a stronger bond than the certification.
- *Place*: increasingly global-facing — India-based coaches courting international ($) clients, or diaspora coaches selling back into India — which is exactly why Uncoded Hub's pricing scales from ₹25–35K toward $5K.

**5-Word Test (hypothesis, needs real DM validation):** "signature program," "discovery call," "high-ticket," "authority positioning," "niche down." Words like "funnel" or "personal brand" leak into every online-business tribe — too broad to count.

**5-Object Test:** a named signature framework/methodology, an ICF or equivalent certification badge, a Calendly link, a Notion-built curriculum, LinkedIn "Top Voice"/Creator Mode status.

**Pains (the fence and the wound):**
- They get referrals but can't scale past their network — no repeatable way to convert cold or warm LinkedIn attention into booked calls.
- A serious prospect Googles them mid-conversation and finds a dated Wix site, a dead Linktree, or nothing — and the deal cools on the spot.
- They're being asked to charge ₹1–5 lakh (or $2–10K) packages while looking, online, like a $500 Fiverr gig.
- They don't understand SEO, chatbots, or "AI," feel behind, and are quietly afraid a client will ask about it.
- Time poverty: they are the delivery mechanism of their own business, so a DIY website project never gets finished.

**Named enemy (the Fence):** not "self-doubt" — it's *generic template websites and Fiverr/Wix DIY builds that make a ₹1L-package coach look like a ₹500-gig freelancer*, and the SEO/ads agencies that overpromise leads and ghost after month one.

**Foundation (cost of entry into this tribe):** the certification + the leap of leaving a salary + surviving the first 12 months without a client pipeline. This is a real, painful cost — which is why "I built this from nothing" is a status marker, not small talk.

**Hearth (non-negotiables):** their credibility/reputation is sacred — they will not adopt anything that feels "salesy" or inauthentic to their coaching philosophy. The instant tell of a fake in this tribe: someone who talks about "scaling" and "funnels" but has never actually coached a paying client.

**Where they are in the journey:** past the "figuring out my niche" stage, actively delivering, inconsistent revenue, ready to raise prices and go after bigger (often international) clients — but self-image and infrastructure haven't caught up to the price point they want to charge.

**Objections to expect:** "I can build this myself on Wix/Framer," "isn't a chatbot impersonal for something as human as coaching," "how is this different from a cheap freelancer," "will this actually bring clients or just look nice," "₹35K/$5K feels like a lot for a website."

**What builds trust in Deepak specifically:** public teardowns of real (anonymized) coach websites showing exactly where they leak credibility; before/after case studies with a stated outcome (e.g., "went from a Linktree to a ₹3L retainer client within 6 weeks"); demos of the AI chatbot actually qualifying and booking a lead live; Deepak naming the enemy out loud (template sites, ghosting agencies) rather than vague "grow your brand" talk; visible proof he's coached-with/served real coaches, not just designed sites.

## Part 3 — Implications: turning 14,500 connections into a tribe

1. **Speak in their words, not marketing words.** Swap "grow your brand" and "increase leads" for "signature program," "discovery call," "high-ticket positioning," "niching down." Generic SaaS-marketing language is what makes a post scroll past unrecognized; tribal language is what makes someone stop and think "that's me."

2. **Name the enemy publicly and often.** A recurring content series — "website teardown of the week" or "why your Fiverr site is costing you a ₹1L client" — gives the tribe something external to rally against, which is far more shareable than "here's our service."

3. **Post proof from inside the room, not outside it.** Case studies with real numbers (packages sold, calls booked, price increases achieved) beat generic "we build websites" posts — they let a coach see their own possible future, which is what triggers recognition and DMs.

4. **Turn passive connections into an identifiable "we."** Right now 14,500 connections are an audience, not a Front Room — there's no named group, no "we" language. Consider naming the community (e.g., "The Coach Authority Circle") so members can identify each other and self-select as insiders, not just followers.

5. **Replace broadcast posting with tribal DMs.** Use the dossier's pains/enemy/objections as DM openers to specific connections ("Saw you're niching into executive coaching — is your current site pulling its weight for ₹2L+ clients?") rather than a cold pitch. This is the fastest way to validate which rooms of the dossier are real versus assumed, and each reply is free market research that sharpens the next post.
