# Lead Magnet Framework — Distilled for Uncoded Hub
*Source: "The Lead Magnet Playbook" (Seven Figure Creators). Distilled for Deepak, applied to the Coach Authority System (website + AI chatbot + SEO, ₹25,000–35,000 scaling toward $5,000, sold to coaches/consultants via ~14,500 LinkedIn connections).*

## The Core Idea

A lead magnet's only job is to build trust *before* the sales conversation starts. Give real value upfront, prove you already understand the prospect's problem and already have the answer, and the DM conversation that follows isn't a cold pitch — it's a warm continuation of value you already delivered. Since "comment X for the resource" is already Deepak's best-performing post pattern, this book is the missing half: what the resource itself needs to be, and how to structure the whole comment → connect → DM → offer pipeline around it.

## Part 1: The 5 Rules of Value (the selection filter)

A lead magnet's worth is entirely subjective — it's worth exactly what the prospect *thinks* it's worth. Run every idea through these five dimensions:

1. **Scarcity** — Does it feel like insider knowledge, not something they could just Google? ("The exact system we use to close $5K website deals — not shared publicly.")
2. **Relevance** — Does it solve *one specific* problem for a specific ICP, not a broad topic? Specificity beats breadth every time.
3. **Immediate Utility** — Can they use it and get value in minutes, with no learning curve?
4. **Time Savings** — Does it shortcut effort, decision-making, or research they'd otherwise have to do themselves?
5. **Money** — Can you tie it to a quantifiable financial outcome (revenue gained, cost avoided, deals saved)?

**Checklist:** the best lead magnets hit all five. If an idea only hits one or two, it's weak — go back and sharpen it before building anything.

## Part 2: What Kills Lead Magnets (avoid these)

- **Starting with the format.** "I'll make an ebook / checklist / quiz" is the single most emphasized mistake in the book — decide the *value* first, format is the last decision, not the first.
- **Irrelevant lead magnets.** Chasing likes/comments/followers with something disconnected from your actual offer attracts the wrong crowd and never converts to sales, no matter how viral the post gets.
- **Being lazy.** A thin resource with a flashy hook gets downloaded once and destroys trust the moment people open it — worse than not offering anything.
- **Analysis paralysis or the "Hail Mary."** Either you overthink and never ship, or you spend weeks building the "perfect" thing untested — and it flops, killing your confidence in lead magnets altogether.

## Part 3: Turning What You Already Know Into a Lead Magnet

1. **Nail the ICP first.** Everything downstream depends on knowing exactly who this is for.
2. **Look at what you're already doing.** What are you solving right now (even for yourself/your own clients)? What do people keep asking you? What have you tested that works?
3. **Slice off one piece — don't teach everything.** Pick the one thing you already deliver, that consistently gets results, that people would pay just to know.
4. **Two paths to build it:** *Innovate/Repurpose* (package your own existing knowledge or tools into something new) or *Curate* ("steal like an artist" — aggregate best practices/frameworks from others, add your spin, credit them if it boosts credibility). Combining both is where the best lead magnets come from.

**Idea-framing template** (use this to test if an idea is sharp enough to build):
> "The [Lead Magnet Name] is for [ICP] struggling to [specific problem]. It shows you how to [secret sauce] in [time saved] with [immediate utility] — [money saved/made] while solving [relevance to pain point]."

If you can't fill that sentence in cleanly, the idea isn't specific enough yet.

**Final 5-point filter before building:** (1) Can you frame it clearly with the template above? (2) Does it solve a problem your ICP actually cares about? (3) Does it hit the 5 Rules of Value? (4) Does it align with what you actually sell (your lead magnet solves *part* of the problem, your paid offer solves the rest)? (5) Can you build it fast without losing value — if it feels heavy, pick a lighter idea that delivers the same value.

## Part 4: Validate Before You Build

Unless it's something you can repurpose in under 1–2 hours, **test demand before creating anything.** 99% of people skip this — it's why most lead magnets flop. Five ways to validate:

1. **Research & conversations** — mine what your ICP already says in real conversations, LinkedIn comments, forums, or search queries.
2. **Test in DMs with pre-loaded value** — float the idea directly to prospects: *"Sounds like you're wrestling with [problem] — we've nailed this so well we're thinking of packaging it into a [format] to give away. Want first dibs?"*
3. **Test in your LinkedIn Featured section** — place a preview there and see if it gets clicks before building the full thing.
4. **Post a teaser / waitlist** — gauge interest with an "early access" post before the resource exists.
5. **Test via a squeeze page** — if your website already gets traffic, a simple landing page tells you fast whether people want it.

## Part 5: Choosing a Format

**Format genuinely doesn't matter**, as long as it (1) clearly communicates the value and is easy to consume, and (2) is easy, fast, and comfortable for *you* to create. Play to your strengths — write if writing is easy, record video if you speak well, design if design is your edge. Categories the book lists: written (guides, checklists, case studies, scripts/templates, audit tools), visual (infographics, process maps, Figma templates), video/audio (walkthroughs, teardowns, mini-courses), interactive (quizzes, calculators, scorecards, worksheets), live (workshops, AMAs, strategy calls), and tool/resource (spreadsheets, dashboards, prompt libraries, swipe files). Notably, **audit tools, scorecards, and calculators are explicitly named formats** — directly usable for a website/chatbot/SEO agency.

## Part 6: Structuring the Content

Every lead magnet, regardless of format, follows one outline:

1. **Explanatory notes / intro** — what it is, why it's useful, a hook or story, the outcome they'll get, how to use it fast.
2. **Main content** — deliver the actual insight/framework step-by-step, tie it back to your paid offer, include a soft nudge toward next steps (no hard sell yet).
3. **Selling next steps** — a short bio (why listen to you), social proof, a clear CTA, optional links.

**Templates for that final section:**
- Credibility: *"Since launching [X], we've helped [N] [ICP] achieve [result]."*
- Qualification: *"This works best for [ICP] who are [stage/problem]. If that's you, here's the next step..."*
- CTA options: book a call / follow for more / download a deeper resource.
- Optional urgency: *"DM me '[keyword]' in the next [timeframe] for [bonus]."*

Keep it short, actionable, and framed as a natural next step — never a hard pitch.

## Part 7: The Comment → DM → Delivery Flow (the mechanism Deepak is already running)

This is the direct playbook for the pattern that already works for him. Every high-performing post follows the same shape:

1. **Hook** — a pattern-interrupt opener: *"I spent [X hours] breaking down how [person/company] achieved [outcome]"* or *"I've helped [audience] achieve [outcome] using [tool] — now I'm giving it away for free."*
2. **What's inside** — 3–4 bullets naming specific insights/tools/outcomes, not vague promises.
3. **The ask** — exactly two frictionless steps: **(1) Comment "[keyword]"** and **(2) Connect with me** (DMs only reach connections on LinkedIn, so this line matters mechanically, not just rhetorically).
4. **Delivery promise** — "I'll send it directly to your DMs."
5. **Bonus/repost lever (optional)** — "Repost this and I'll include [extra bonus]" — extends reach for free.

Three named templates: *Authority & Transformation*, *Lead Magnet Teaser*, and *Results-Oriented Case Study* — same skeleton, different opening frame. Pick whichever fits the specific magnet.

**Visuals are non-negotiable.** Every post needs a supporting image or GIF previewing the resource, mobile-optimized (~800×1000px so it fills the phone viewport). This is treated as equally important as the copy.

**Once in the DM — the "sell-by-chat" logic:** the fastest way to build trust in a chat isn't *telling* someone you can help, it's *showing* them — deliver the lead magnet as promised, then use "preloaded value" (extra relevant insight dropped in-chat) to move skepticism → curiosity → trust → booked call. The lead magnet is the opening move of a conversation, not the end of one.

**Evergreen layer:** don't rely only on one-off posts — put a preview of the lead magnet in your **LinkedIn Featured section** with a clear "Get the free guide" CTA, and back it with a dedicated **squeeze page** on your website. This captures traffic between viral posts and compounds every future post's effect.

## Part 8: 3–5 Lead Magnet Ideas for the Coach Authority System

Applying the 5 Rules + format logic to Uncoded Hub's ICP (coaches/consultants who need a website, AI chatbot, and SEO):

1. **"The 12-Point Coach Website Audit Checklist"** (written/audit-tool format). *Relevance*: names the exact things coaches get wrong (no booking CTA, no chatbot, invisible on Google). *Immediate utility*: they can self-score their own site today. *Money*: frame it as "each unchecked box is likely costing you booked calls."
2. **"The AI Chatbot Readiness Scorecard"** (interactive quiz/calculator). Prospect answers 8–10 questions about their current site/funnel, gets a score + 2–3 specific gaps. This is the strongest fit for the book's "interactive" category and doubles as a soft-sell into the chatbot component of the package — the scorecard itself demonstrates the AI capability being sold.
3. **"5-Minute Website + SEO Teardown"** (Loom video, personalized). Record a short teardown of a prospect's actual site as the "resource," offered via DM after they comment — this maps directly to the book's "test in DMs with pre-loaded value" tactic and the sell-by-chat approach: show, don't tell.
4. **"The Coach's DM Script Swipe File"** (template/script bank). Copy-paste scripts coaches can use to convert their own website/LinkedIn traffic into booked calls — high perceived value because it's outside Uncoded Hub's core service but adjacent to the outcome coaches want (more clients), building authority broadly.
5. **"The Coach Authority System Blueprint"** (one-page visual/process map). A simple before/after diagram showing how website + chatbot + SEO combine into one client-acquisition system — this is the "evergreen" magnet for the LinkedIn Featured section, positioned as the entry point of the value ladder toward the paid package.

Each of these should be run through validation first (float it in a few DMs or as a Featured-section preview) before full production, per Part 4.

## The Single Biggest Mistake This Book Warns About

**Starting with the format instead of the value.** The book calls this out first, in the strongest language it uses anywhere ("STOP. DON'T DO THIS."): deciding "I'll make an ebook / checklist / quiz" before figuring out whether the underlying content actually solves one specific, urgent problem for a named ICP. Format-first thinking produces polished resources nobody needed. The book's fix is sequence: ICP → the one problem → the 5 Rules of Value → *then* pick whichever format is easiest for you to ship. The closely-related second failure mode — skipping validation and either freezing (analysis paralysis) or over-building a "perfect" untested magnet (the Hail Mary) — is treated as the second-biggest and gets the most page space in the book, since 99% of creators skip it.
