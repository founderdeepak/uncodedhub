*Source: Matthew Lakajev, "How To Niche And Make F\*ck You Money Online" (21 parts, ~421KB / ~23k words of core content, read in full). Distilled against the existing Niche Scoring Bible / Tribal Scoring Engine verdict on "coaches and consultants globally."*

---

## 1. The book's core framework: "Niching Physics"

Lakajev's entire book collapses into one formula, restated a dozen ways across 21 parts:

**Niche Resonance = Identity Match × Language Match × Cultural Match × Trust Utility**

His argument, stripped of the repetition: a "niche" is not a market segment, an industry, a job title, or a demographic. It is **a moment of recognition** — the instant a specific reader thinks *"holy shit, that's me"* and, because of that, extends trust. Everything else (content, DMs, sales calls, offers) either produces that moment or it doesn't. He calls this reaction the single KPI that matters, and multiplication (not addition) is the operative math: if any one factor is zero, the whole thing collapses — a good offer aimed at nobody's identity converts nobody.

The four factors, broken into his sub-frameworks:

**The Identity Engine — six layers that make someone recognize themselves:**
1. **Subculture** — the tribe/community identity ("what type of person am I")
2. **Local culture** — geography as worldview, not just location (a Mumbai-raised founder in Sydney behaves differently than one raised there)
3. **Temporal identity** — the *season of life* someone is in (new parent, mid-career plateau, post-divorce, pre-exit)
4. **Psychic identity** — the private, unspoken internal loop (guilt, self-doubt, a fear they've never said out loud)
5. **Aspirational identity** — who they're quietly trying to become, not who they currently are
6. **Algorithmic identity** — the content cluster the platform already believes they belong to (feeds are identity fingerprints now, not broadcast media)

**The Linguistic Engine — five language types that signal "I'm one of you":**
1. Functional language (the verbs of what they actually do)
2. Technical language (insider jargon/acronyms only a member of the world would know)
3. Cultural language (lifestyle references, humor, environment)
4. Psychic language (their private self-talk, verbatim)
5. Narrative language (the recurring plot/cycle their life follows)

**The Cultural Engine — four contexts that shape what feels normal, urgent, or aspirational:** local culture, occupational culture, digital microculture, and trend culture (the current identity-wave, e.g., a fitness/wellness trend someone is orbiting).

**Trust Utility Equation** (borrowed and reframed as the buyer-side mechanism): Trust Utility = P(outcome) × Value − Risk. A generic niche statement collapses all three variables at once: the reader can't picture the outcome applying to *their* specific life (low P(outcome)), the outcome feels undefined so it's not clearly valuable, and vagueness itself reads as risk ("if they help everyone, they probably help nobody well").

The book's diagnostic mechanism for validation is the **Recognition Test**: applied not to a one-time niche statement but continuously, to every post, DM, and call — "does the specific person I'm talking to involuntarily feel described?" He also names a **Seven Deadly Sins of Niching** list, and the first three sins are, in spirit: niching by job title, niching by vague pain, and niching by industry.

## 2. How this compares to the existing Tribal Scoring Engine verdict

**Strong, direct agreement — no tension.** The two frameworks arrive at the same conclusion from different angles, and this book actually sharpens the diagnosis of *why* "coaches and consultants globally" scores near the floor (57–68/1000):

- The Tribal Scoring Engine calls it a "demographic, not a tribe." Lakajev's framework says the same thing in his own vocabulary: "coach" and "consultant" are **job titles**, and job titles are explicitly named as a niching sin because nobody's internal identity runs on their job title — no one lies awake thinking "as a coach, I feel X." A job title has zero subculture, zero local culture, zero life-stage specificity, zero psychic identity, and zero algorithmic identity attached to it, so all four multipliers in the Niche Resonance formula land near zero simultaneously. This is a structural, not incidental, failure — exactly matching the tribal-scoring finding that this "niche" generates near-zero inbound DMs.
- Lakajev's algorithmic-identity layer adds a mechanistic explanation the Tribal Scoring Engine doesn't spell out: platforms no longer distribute content to broad categories at all. There is no "coaches feed." A LinkedIn post aimed at "coaches globally" has no identity cluster for the algorithm to push it into, so distribution — not just resonance — collapses. This means the weak DM/inbound performance isn't only a messaging problem; it's partly a distribution problem caused by the same root cause (no coherent tribe to target).
- His prescription is identical in direction to the Tribal Scoring Engine's recommendation: don't abandon coaching, **attach it to a pre-existing, already-cohering world** — which is exactly the shape of the four sub-tribe examples already on the table (an industry-vertical executive-coach community, licensed professionals moving into advisory, a circumstantial cohort like ex-corporate professionals, a certification community). In Lakajev's terms, each of those four candidates already carries at least one strong "subculture" and "occupational culture" layer built in — which is why they scored higher in the first place.

**Where the book adds nuance / extends the existing verdict, rather than just re-confirming it:**

1. **It elevates geography/local culture to a first-class axis, arguably underused in a global-tribal-scoring approach.** Lakajev treats "where someone is from" (city, even suburb) as equal in power to occupational or certification identity — not just a modifier. For Uncoded Hub this suggests a sub-niche doesn't have to be purely occupational (e.g., "healthcare-vertical executive coaches") — it could be occupational *plus* geographic (e.g., "executive coaches serving Tier-1-city Indian SaaS leadership," or "ex-corporate professionals-turned-coaches in the Gulf/NRI market" if Deepak's network skews that way). This is a genuinely new lever the tribal scoring verdict's four examples don't foreground.

2. **It reframes "psychic identity" as the highest-leverage layer for coaches specifically** — the private, never-said-out-loud internal loop. For a coaching sub-niche, this means the winning positioning line won't be built from what the tribe *does* (functional language) but from what they secretly feel and never admit (e.g., not "I coach ex-corporate professionals" but something that names the specific 2am thought that tribe has and doesn't say to anyone — guilt about the identity loss, fear of being "found out" as unqualified to advise, etc.). This is a concrete positioning-copy technique, not just a targeting rule.

3. **It reframes niche selection as iterative, not a one-time analytical score.** The Tribal Scoring Engine appears to be a point-in-time diagnostic (a score out of 1000). Lakajev's "Evolution of a Niche" chapter argues a niche is never "solved" — it sharpens through direct client contact, DM replies, and content feedback over time. This doesn't contradict the scoring approach; it argues the score should be treated as a *starting hypothesis to test in market this week*, not a final verdict to over-analyze before shipping anything.

4. **It gives an alternate, faster discovery method alongside analytical scoring: "accidental niching."** Rather than only reasoning top-down about which tribe to target, the book's "Fuck It Moment" chapter argues the fastest path to an accurate niche is mining what you *already* say unfiltered — your own lived professional history, the specific frustrations you already vent about, the specific type of person who already replies to your existing posts. For Deepak this is directly actionable: audit his own past year of LinkedIn posts/DMs for which specific comments came from which specific sub-tribe, rather than starting purely from a hypothesis.

**No real tension found.** Nothing in the book argues for staying broad, and nothing contradicts the recommendation to narrow to a pre-existing tribe. If anything, it argues the tribal-scoring verdict is directionally conservative — hyper-specific, even single-city or single-employer-history niches, are framed as *winning*, not as a compromise you make to get *some* score above the floor.

## 3. What it adds beyond what's already known: pricing/monetization angle

The book contains **no explicit pricing framework or tiered-pricing methodology** — this is a real gap relative to what Deepak might want. The one relevant mechanism is indirect but useful: because **Value** in the Trust Utility equation is contextual (the same outcome is worth wildly different amounts depending on how urgently/personally that specific world feels it), a tightly-matched sub-niche can credibly support **higher pricing with lower perceived risk** than a generic "coach website package" pitch — because P(outcome) is higher and Risk is lower for that specific buyer. Practically: once Deepak picks a winning sub-niche, the Coach Authority System pitch to that tribe should be repriced/re-anchored to what that specific world's transformation is worth to *them* (e.g., "ex-corporate professionals moving into paid advisory" may value credibility-signaling infrastructure — website + AI chatbot + SEO — far more urgently than "coaches globally" would, justifying the higher end of the ₹25–35k band or the $5,000 scaling price sooner). This is a rationale for **differential pricing by sub-niche**, not a formula for what to charge.

## 4. Concrete step-by-step: landing on 1–2 sub-niche candidates this week

Combining the existing Tribal Scoring Engine shortlist with this book's Identity/Linguistic Engine and Recognition Test as a live validation layer:

**Day 1 — Mine existing signal, don't start from a blank page.**
Audit Deepak's own ~14,500 LinkedIn connections and the last 6–12 months of post comments/DMs. Tag connections by: (a) certification body (ICF, EMCC, etc.), (b) prior employer/industry vertical, (c) how they describe their transition into coaching in their own bio language, (d) geography. Cross-reference against which past posts got the most *specific* ("this is literally me") comments versus generic likes. This surfaces 4–6 candidate sub-tribes empirically, not hypothetically — likely landing on the same shape as the existing four examples (vertical executive coaches, licensed-professionals-turned-advisors, ex-corporate cohort, certification community) but with Deepak's *actual* network data behind each.

**Day 2 — Build an Identity + Language profile per candidate (not a persona).**
For each of the 4–6 candidates, fill out one paragraph per Identity Engine layer (subculture, local culture, life-stage, private psychic loop, aspiration, algorithmic content cluster they already consume) and one column per Linguistic Engine type (their functional verbs, technical jargon, cultural references, private self-talk, and the recurring "story" of their week/year). This is the raw material for positioning language — the goal is a lived-world description, not a bio.

**Day 3 — Turn each profile into live test content and DM openers.**
Draft 2–3 LinkedIn posts and 2 cold-DM openers per candidate, written entirely in that tribe's own language (psychic + technical + narrative, no job titles, no "coaches/consultants"). Publish across the week, staggered by candidate so responses can be attributed.

**Day 4–6 — Run the Recognition Test empirically instead of guessing.**
Track, per candidate: comment specificity (a generic "great post!" vs. a specific "this is literally my Tuesday"), DM reply rate, and whether replies volunteer more of their own story unprompted (a strong Trust Utility signal — Risk has already dropped). This is a cheap, fast proxy for the "holy shit that's me" reaction the book treats as the only real test, and it can run in parallel with (not instead of) re-scoring each candidate against the existing Tribal Scoring Engine's 25 mechanisms.

**Day 7 — Pick 1–2 winners and re-anchor the Coach Authority System pitch.**
Choose the sub-niche(s) with the strongest combined signal (tribal score + live recognition signal), then rewrite the Coach Authority System landing page, chatbot prompts, and DM scripts using that tribe's own technical/psychic/narrative language rather than generic "coach" copy — and reconsider pricing upward for that tribe specifically, since a matched niche should tolerate a higher price than the broad, unscored "coaches globally" pitch ever could.

**Bottom line:** this book doesn't just agree with the "coaches globally is too broad" verdict — it explains the mechanism (job-title niches fail the Identity, Language, Culture, and Trust tests simultaneously, and get zero algorithmic distribution) and supplies a language-level toolkit (Identity Engine + Linguistic Engine) plus a live, cheap validation method (the Recognition Test run on real posts/DMs this week) that the tribal-scoring verdict, being a static analytical score, doesn't include on its own.
