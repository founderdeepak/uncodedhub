# Sell-By-Chat Conversion — Distilled from Steve Butler's Playbook

**Source:** *The Sell-By-Chat Playbook: Turn DMs into Cash On LinkedIn* by Steve Butler
**Purpose:** Supplement to Uncoded Hub's existing LinkedIn DM framework (cold/intent/inbound DM types, 7-part cold DM template, Trust Equity = Time × Touches × Type, ~26 touches to a booked call). This doc covers everything the book teaches about what happens **inside the conversation once someone replies** — qualifying, objection handling, closing, and follow-up — which the existing framework doesn't detail.

---

## 1. The Core Philosophy: Serve, Don't Sell

The book's one non-negotiable mindset shift: **stop selling, start serving.** Every message should feel like you're solving a problem the prospect already told you about (directly or via their posts/activity), not pushing an offer. The sale is framed as "the side effect of being genuinely helpful," not the goal of the message.

Three excuses the book explicitly tells you to kill:
- *"It feels salesy"* — only true if you're spamming canned pitches. Real listening doesn't feel salesy.
- *"Leads go cold"* — they're not cold, they're unfollowed-up. Most deals close on touch 5+.
- *"Outsourcing doesn't work"* — it does, but only with a rock-solid, written process (which is the point of this doc).

**Chat-to-Contract Ratio** (the book's core KPI, useful to benchmark Deepak's own funnel against):

| Stage | Rate | Cumulative (from 100 chats) |
|---|---|---|
| Reply | 30% | 30 |
| Meaningful engagement | 50% | 15 |
| Call booked | 20% | 3 |
| Show-up | 80% | 2.4 |
| Close | 50% | 1.2 |

~1% of cold contacts become closed deals. Daily baseline: 25-30 contacts/day → ~1 call booked/day → 2-3 sales/week. Track this ratio to diagnose *where* Deepak's funnel leaks (reply problem vs. qualifying problem vs. show-up problem vs. close problem) rather than treating "low sales" as one undifferentiated problem.

## 2. The Conversation Flow, Stage by Stage

This is the connective tissue the existing DM framework is missing — what to actually *do* once a prospect responds.

### Stage 1 — Opener (post-connection or warm-signal trigger)
- The opener is never about you — it's a **noticing**, not an icebreaker.
- **Precision flattery**: don't praise outcomes, praise effort/thinking ("Your approach to [X] is EPIC because…") — proves you actually read their content, not just skimmed it.
- **Pattern interrupt**: say something specific + emotionally resonant + relevant (a sharp analogy, a niche detail only an insider would notice). Never generic ("let's connect," "picking your brain").
- For **warm signals** (profile view, like, comment, repost, new connection), always give the prospect **two named paths** to reply to, reducing decision friction:
  - Profile view: *"Hey [Name], noticed you checked out my profile. Just curious — are you looking for [outcome], or just enjoying the content?"*
  - Like: *"Thanks for liking my post on [topic] — are you working on [related challenge], or did it just resonate?"*

### Stage 2 — Qualifying (the missing middle of Deepak's funnel)
Two frameworks, used together:

**LVQ Framework (Matt Lakajev)** — the rhythm of every qualifying exchange:
- **Lube** — acknowledge what they said first ("Got it," "That makes sense") to keep friction at zero.
- **Value** — give a genuinely useful insight/tip *before* asking anything.
- **Question** — one open-ended question that digs one level deeper.

**A–B Method** — qualify by finding and *widening* the gap:
- **Point A**: where are they now / what's the specific pain?
- **Point B**: where do they want to be / what does success look like?
- The bigger that gap feels to *them* (not to you), the more urgency they self-generate. Use questions to make them articulate the gap out loud rather than you stating it.

**Question sequencing rule**: start broad → go deeper → test readiness. Never stack questions ("How's business? What's challenging? What's your strategy?"). One question per message, always.

Binary/either-or questions ("was it [A] or [B]?") consistently outperform open "what's wrong?" questions for reply rate, because they lower the cognitive cost of replying.

Common qualifying mistakes to avoid: qualifying too early/hard, skipping the emotional "gap," and talking more than listening.

### Stage 3 — Preloaded Value (trust-building before the ask)
Before asking for a call or a sale, deliver something specific, ungated, and immediately actionable — not a generic PDF. Rules:
1. **Specific** to their world/industry/recent post — not generic advice.
2. **Ungated** — no opt-in wall, no "sign up to get it." Free means trustworthy.
3. **Actionable** — a checklist, single reframe, or mini-framework they can use today.
4. **Tied to the next step** — solve one piece of their puzzle so they come back for the rest.

This activates **reciprocity**: instead of you chasing the sale, they start chasing the solution. It also primes call attendance — send a teaser/case-study *before* a booked call so they show up ready to listen, not evaluate.

### Stage 4 — Guiding the Conversation with Micro-Commitments
The engine that moves a qualified chat toward a booked call. Five tactics, all designed to make "yes" the path of least resistance:

1. **Make the ask small and specific**: *"Let's lock in a quick 10-minute call — does [day/time] work?"* (never open-ended "want to hop on a call sometime?")
2. **Build emotional momentum — frame work as already underway**: *"I've already started mapping out a few ideas we've used to solve [X] for others."* This is the book's most distinctive lever: it manufactures a sense of investment/reciprocity *before* the prospect has committed anything, making it psychologically harder to back out.
3. **Link the ask to a tangible outcome**: tie the call/next step to a concrete result, not "let's chat."
4. **Break big decisions into small steps**: "Why don't I send over the details first, then we talk?" — removes pressure to commit upfront.
5. **Always tie back to their stated value/result**, never to your process.

### Stage 5 — Objection Handling: "No BUTs"
A three-step sequence, plus one language rule:

1. **Acknowledge** — *"I hear you, timing is always tricky with [X]."* Disarms defensiveness.
2. **Ask for context** — *"What's your biggest concern about [issue] right now?"* / *"What would need to change for this to feel right?"* Positions you as curious, not pushy — and usually surfaces the *real* objection (which is rarely the stated one).
3. **Reassure with proof** — *"One of my clients felt exactly the same about [X]. After [action], they got [result]. I think it'd work for you too."*

**The "No BUTs" rule**: never use "but" after acknowledging a concern — it silently cancels the empathy that came before it. Replace with "and," or drop the connector and restate positively.
- Bad: "I understand your concern, but this could solve it."
- Good: "I understand your concern, and I think this approach could make it easier."

This is a small language habit worth building into any scripted objection-handling responses Deepak's team uses.

### Stage 6 — Closing the Loop (ending every chat with intent)
Every single conversation — even ones that don't convert — should end with a deliberate next step, never left ambiguous. Four closing patterns:

1. **Explicit next step tied to their stated challenge**: *"I'd love to share a quick framework that's helped others in your position — how does [day/time] sound for 15 minutes?"*
2. **Remove decision fatigue by proposing, not asking**: *"I've penciled in [specific day/time] for us to call — let me know if that doesn't work and I'll adjust."* **Skip the calendar link entirely** — the book explicitly calls booking tools a source of friction and lost momentum. Handle scheduling directly in-chat.
3. **Play the long game when timing is genuinely wrong**: end warmly, name a specific re-check-in window, leave the door open — don't disqualify a "not now" as a "no."
4. **Circle back with a value recap** for warm-but-uncommitted leads: restate their challenge + your proposed solution + one clear next step in a single message.

### Stage 7 — Booking → Show-Up (reducing no-shows)
- Offer times **within the next 3 business days** — further out kills momentum and enthusiasm.
- Send reminders at **24 hours and 1 hour** before the call, each carrying fresh value (not just "see you tomorrow"): reference a case study, tease an insight, or show "we've already started prepping for your call."
- Frame the call as low-pressure ("15-minute strategy chat," not "sales call").

### Stage 8 — Closing to Payment
The book's actual closing/payment script (short, momentum-based, and worth adapting directly for Deepak's post-call follow-through):

> *"Excited to get started! Once payment is set, we can kick off [specific next step]. Need any help with that?"*

Why it works: it assumes the sale is already happening ("excited to get started," not "would you like to proceed"), it names a concrete deliverable that starts *only after* payment (creating urgency to pay), and it proactively offers to remove payment friction rather than waiting for the prospect to raise it.

## 3. Follow-Up Cadence

The book's stated fact: **80% of sales happen after the 5th touchpoint** — most people quit before that. Recommended cadence for a lead who's gone quiet:

- **Day 1**: follow up the day after first message/call.
- **Day 3**: a light check-in.
- **Day 7**: a "still here" nudge.
- **After day 7**: space follow-ups every couple of weeks to avoid burning the lead, rather than stopping entirely.

This is a *shorter-range, chat-specific* cadence that complements (doesn't replace) Deepak's existing ~26-touch, longer-horizon Trust Equity model — use this cadence for warm/qualifying conversations already underway, and the 26-touch model for cold relationship-building before first real engagement.

Follow-up message patterns:
- **Personal-touch revival**: *"Remember how we talked about [specific challenge]? I just helped a client with something similar and the results were bananas — want me to share what we did?"*
- **Fresh-value revival**: *"I just worked with someone in a similar spot and thought of you — want to hear how we made it work?"*
- **Low-effort close-out**: *"Want me to send the details?"*

Practical tracking: keep pipeline stages simple (New Leads → Awaiting Reply → Replied → Qualifying → Call Booked → Closed-Won/Lost) and log notes per conversation (last message direction, next step, budget/timeline/key problem mentioned) so follow-ups don't require re-reading the whole thread each time.

## 4. Quick-Reference Script Skeletons (adaptable to Coach Authority System)

- **Post-connection opener**: notice something specific → one binary qualifying question.
- **Qualifying loop**: Lube (acknowledge) → Value (one insight) → Question (one open question, A→B framing).
- **Preloaded value drop**: one specific, ungated, actionable resource tied to their stated pain, ending with a soft next-step tie-in.
- **Objection response**: Acknowledge → Ask why → Reassure with a specific proof point → never use "but."
- **Booking ask**: propose one specific day/time directly (no calendar link) + tie to a tangible result + optional "already in motion" framing.
- **Payment close**: assume the yes, name the next concrete deliverable, offer to remove friction.

## 5. What This Adds Beyond the Existing Framework

The existing LinkedIn skill covers *getting* a reply and *earning* a booked call through repeated touches. This book fills the gap **between "they replied" and "they paid"**: a repeatable qualifying rhythm (LVQ + A-B gap-widening), a specific objection-handling script (Acknowledge → Context → Reassure, no "buts"), momentum-based micro-commitment language for moving people to a call without a booking link, and an explicit, assumptive payment-closing script. It also gives a diagnostic KPI ladder (Chat-to-Contract Ratio) to identify exactly which stage of Deepak's funnel is underperforming.
