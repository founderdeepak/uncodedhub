## Step 1: The Core NAP & Category Hierarchy

Google’s ranking algorithm prioritizes three core factors: **Relevance, Distance, and Prominence**. Relevance begins with your core category configuration.

### 1.1 Business Title Formatting (The White-Hat Rule)
- **Do**: Use your exact registered business name: e.g., `Meridian Architecture & Interior Studio`.
- **Warning on Keyword Stuffing**: Avoid stuffing keywords into your official business name (e.g., `Meridian Architecture - Best Cheap Interior Designers Bangalore HSR`). Google’s anti-spam algorithm actively penalizes keyword-stuffed names with immediate suspensions.
- **The Pro Tactic**: If your legal name includes your core descriptor, use it consistently across your tax registration, website footer, and storefront signage.

### 1.2 Category Selection (Your #1 Relevance Lever)
Google allows **one Primary Category** and up to **nine Secondary Categories**. Your primary category carries approximately 60% of your relevance weighting:
- **Primary Category**: Must match your core revenue-generating service (e.g., `Interior Designer`, `Dental Clinic`, `Wedding Photographer`, `Car Detailing Service`).
- **Secondary Categories**: Add 3 to 5 related secondary categories that capture adjacent searches:
  - For Interior Designers: `Architectural Designer`, `Kitchen Remodeler`, `Furniture Store`.
  - For Dental Clinics: `Cosmetic Dentist`, `Orthodontist`, `Emergency Dental Service`.
  - For Car Detailing: `Car Wash`, `Auto Restoration Service`, `Window Tinting Service`.

## Step 2: Service Areas & Address Verification

### 2.1 Storefront vs. Service Area Business (SAB)
- If clients visit your physical practice/studio: Display your full verified address, suite number, and postal code.
- If you travel to clients (contractors, caterers, photographers): You can hide your residential address and define your **Service Areas**.
- **The Service Area Rule**: Define 10 to 15 specific localities/suburbs surrounding your physical location (e.g., `Koramangala`, `Indiranagar`, `HSR Layout`, `Whitefield`). Do not claim an entire country if you operate locally; hyper-focused local boundaries rank significantly higher.

## Step 3: Complete Service Menu & Pricing Architecture

Most businesses leave their GBP Services section completely empty or fill only 2 items. Google indexes the text inside your Services tab to answer conversational search queries.

### 3.1 The Service Listing Formula
For every service listed on your new Uncoded Hub website:
1. **Service Item Name**: Exact match with your website sub-service (e.g., `Full Modular Kitchen Design`).
2. **Service Price**: Set a realistic starting price (e.g., `Starts from ₹1,50,000`). Transparent pricing increases click-through rates by 28%.
3. **Service Description (300 Words Max)**: Write a clear paragraph explaining what is included, who it is for, and incorporating local landmarks or locality names naturally.

## Step 5: Citation Consistency & Off-Page Signals

Google crawls the public internet to verify that your business details match across third-party directories. Any discrepancy in your phone number or address causes "NAP Confusion" and drops your map rankings.

### Top Indian & Global Directories to Secure:
1. Google Business Profile (Primary)
2. Bing Places for Business
3. Apple Maps / Apple Business Connect
4. Justdial & Sulekha (Keep NAP identical; decline paid sales calls)
5. IndiaMART & TradeIndia (For B2B & Wholesale manufacturers)
6. LinkedIn Company Page & Facebook Professional Page
7. Practo / Lybrate (For Clinics & Doctors)
8. Houzz / Architectural Digest Directory (For Architects & Designers)

## Step 6: Weekly GBP Posts & Q&A Seeding

### 6.1 The Weekly Update Post
Treat GBP like a micro-blog. Publish **one GBP Post every Monday morning**:
- **Format**: 1 High-resolution photo + 100-word update + Call to Action button (`Book`, `Call Now`, or `Learn More` linking directly to your Uncoded Hub website).
- Highlight a recent client win, a seasonal offer, or an educational tip.

### 6.2 Pre-Seeding the Questions & Answers (Q&A) Section
Anyone can ask and answer questions on your Google listing. Do not wait for strangers to ask questions:
1. Log in from your personal Google account.
2. Ask the 5 most common questions your clients ask before booking (e.g., *"Do you offer on-site consultations?", "What are your payment terms?"*).
3. Log in as your Business Profile Owner and write authoritative, reassuring answers with direct phone contact details.
