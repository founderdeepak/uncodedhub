HOW TO READ THIS FILE
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Four different things are being optimised for at once, and they need
different keyword shapes:

  SEO  (Search Engine Optimization)   -> ranking in classic Google/Bing
       results. Keyword shape: short, high-volume phrases people type.
       Example: "website designer for interior designers"

  AEO  (Answer Engine Optimization)   -> winning the featured snippet /
       "People Also Ask" box and voice-assistant answers. Keyword shape:
       full questions, phrased the way a person actually asks them.
       Example: "how much does a website cost for a dental clinic in
       Bengaluru"

  GEO  (Generative Engine Optimization) -> being the source an AI
       chatbot (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot) cites or paraphrases
       when someone asks it a question in that tool instead of Google.
       Keyword shape: conversational, multi-clause prompts a person would
       actually type into an AI chat.
       Example: "I'm a wedding photographer in Bangalore, what should my
       website include to get more bookings"

  AIEO (AI Engine Optimization)       -> making sure AI crawlers and
       LLMs can technically read, parse, and correctly summarise the
       site at all (this is the llms.txt + clean HTML + structured data
       layer, not a keyword layer). Handled separately via public/llms.txt
       and structured data — not duplicated in this keyword list.

Every category below gives keyword sets for the first three (SEO, AEO,
GEO), because those three are what blog content is actually written
against. AIEO is a technical foundation (llms.txt is already built —
see public/llms.txt) that makes all the content below easier for AI
systems to find, trust, and cite once it exists.

RECOMMENDED CONTENT STRUCTURE (for when /blog is built)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The sitemap currently keeps /blog out deliberately because it has no
articles yet (see public/sitemap.xml). When posts exist, structure them
under the niche, not under a flat /blog/ list, so each niche builds its
own topical authority instead of diluting into one generic blog:

  /blog/interior-designers/...
  /blog/real-estate/...
  /blog/dental-clinics/...
  /blog/wedding-photographers/...
  /blog/home-renovation/...
  /blog/coaches-consultants/...

Each niche folder should eventually have a single "hub" page that links
to every article in that niche (e.g. /blog/interior-designers/ as an
index) — this is what turns a pile of posts into topical authority and
domain authority, and it is also the page most likely to get cited
whole by an AI answer engine, since it summarises the niche in one place.

Roll niches out one at a time, matching the sequential niche rollout
already locked for the rest of the site (see the design-direction
memory) — do not try to write all six niches' content simultaneously.

HOW THIS BUILDS DOMAIN AUTHORITY
------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Pick one niche at a time (matching the site's own sequential niche
   rollout) and publish its blog hub + 4-6 articles before moving to
   the next niche. A shallow six-niche blog started all at once ranks
   worse than one niche done properly.
2. Every article should link to the matching Services scope and to at
   least one other article in the same niche hub — internal links
   inside one topic cluster are what search engines read as topical
   authority.
3. AEO/GEO keyword phrasing (the full-question and conversational sets
   above) should become actual H2/H3 headings inside the article, not
   just meta descriptions — that is what gets pulled into featured
   snippets and AI-generated answers.
4. Do not fabricate statistics, client counts, or results inside any of
   this content — this project's honesty rule applies to blog content
   exactly as it applies to the rest of the site.
