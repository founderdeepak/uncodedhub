## 4. Call script and questions

### Step 1: Open warmly (2 minutes)

- Thank him for sharing the PDF and say something specific, for example that the wall layout visuals are very strong.
- Say: "I'd like to understand your plans first, and then I'll share how a website could help."

### Step 2: Understand his goals (most important)

- What made you start selling frames, and what do you want to achieve in the next 6 to 12 months?
- Who do you want to sell to: individual buyers, interior designers, builders, hotels, corporate gifting?
- How do you sell today: Instagram, WhatsApp, word of mouth, exhibitions?
- You mentioned selling without much noise. What does that mean to you: selective buyers, a premium feel, or avoiding heavy marketing?
- What would make a website a success for you: enquiries, direct orders, or credibility when you meet a hotel or builder?

### Step 3: Understand the business

- Which brand name should be on the website: Dhananjay Joshi Pure Photography, DJ's Touch, or WildArtBank?
- Do you print the frames yourself or work with a printer? What is the turnaround time and delivery area?
- Are prices fixed, or quoted by size and frame? Do you want prices on the website?
- Do you take bulk or corporate orders? Should there be a section for businesses?
- Do you also want to promote your photography coaching and wildlife tours, or only the frames?

### Step 4: Understand the content

- Which photos should go on the site, and roughly how many (20, 50, 100+)?
- Are the photos available in high resolution, with names or categories?
- Can we use the room layout mockups from the PDF? Are there more?
- Do you have a logo, a short bio and a profile photo?
- Do you already own a domain name (for example wildartbank.com)?
- Do you want any protection for the photos (watermark, no right-click download)?

### Step 5: Understand how people will buy

- Should visitors order online, or send an enquiry by WhatsApp, a form or a call?
- Would a catalogue with an "enquire about this frame" button be enough to begin with?

### Step 6: Understand updates

- How often do you expect to add new photos?
- Do you want to add them yourself, or have us do it for you?

### Step 7: Share your suggestion

Keep it simple and tie it to his worry about noise:

> "A website for you doesn't need to be noisy. It can be a quiet, elegant gallery that shows your frames properly, with an easy way to enquire or order."

Then describe the options you prepared and recommend one. My suggestion is to start with the gallery and enquiry version, because custom sizes, frame types and B2B orders usually need a conversation anyway. Payments can be added later.

### Step 8: Close with next steps

- Repeat back what you heard in two or three lines, so he knows you listened.
- Say you will send a short proposal and name a day (for example, within two days).
- Do not quote a final price on the call unless you and Deepak have already agreed it.

## 5. Pricing guide

These are rough ranges based on typical small-business website pricing in India. Check them against a few local freelancers and studios, and agree the final numbers with Deepak.

| Package | What is included | Rough range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gallery + enquiry (suggested start) | About 5 pages (Home, Gallery, Layout ideas, About, Contact), around 40 to 60 photos, enquiry form and WhatsApp button, mobile-optimized, basic SEO | Rs 15,000 to 30,000 |
| Catalogue + online orders | Everything above, plus sizes and frame options, price display, payment gateway (for example Razorpay), order emails | Rs 35,000 to 70,000 |
| B2B add-on | A page for hotels, builders and corporate gifting, with a bulk enquiry form | Rs 5,000 to 12,000 |

**Recurring costs** (quote these separately so they are not a surprise later):

- Domain and hosting: roughly Rs 3,000 to 8,000 a year, depending on the setup
- Photo updates by you: roughly Rs 1,500 to 4,000 a month, or a pay-per-update rate

**Pricing principles:**

- Price by value, not by hours. Building with AI makes you faster, but he is paying for a finished, reliable site and your 7-day, fixed-scope promise.
- The free offer is closed, so an introductory price is a fair way to land the first client, for example a lower fee in exchange for a testimonial and permission to show the site in your portfolio.
- Take an advance (commonly 40 to 50 percent) before you start, and put what is included and what is not in writing.
- Hold your scope. Online payments, shipping rules and a coaching or tours booking system are separate phases, not part of the base price.

## 6. How he will update his photos

If the site is AI-generated code (plain HTML files), a photographer cannot update it himself, because adding a photo would mean editing code. Decide how updates will work before you build.

- **Option 1: an easy dashboard (best for him).** Build on a CMS-backed setup (WordPress, Webflow, Framer or a simple headless CMS) so he can log in, upload a photo, add a title and size, and publish. AI can still help you design and build it. It takes slightly more setup, but he becomes independent.
- **Option 2: he sends, you update (simplest to deliver).** He shares photos by WhatsApp or a shared folder and you add them within an agreed time, such as 48 hours. Offer this as a paid monthly plan. It also gives you recurring income and a reason to stay in touch.
- **Option 3: folder-based gallery.** The site reads from a cloud folder so he only drops photos in. It is quick for him but needs careful setup and is less reliable, so test it well before choosing it.

**My suggestion:** Option 1 if he wants to manage it himself, with Option 2 as a paid backup.

**Simple photo guidelines to give him:**

- Export at about 2000 pixels on the longest side, under roughly 500 KB to 1 MB per photo, so the site stays fast
- Use JPEG or WebP format
- Name files clearly, for example tiger-bandhavgarh-30x30
- Keep a short note for each photo: title, animal or category, available sizes
- Keep the original high-resolution files private. The website only needs web-sized copies, which also helps protect his work

**On using AI:** you do not need to hide it. If he asks, answer honestly that you use AI tools to build faster and that you review and test everything yourselves. Test the finished site properly (mobile, speed, forms), because that is where AI-built sites most often slip.

## 7. Project plan from start to finish

1. **Proposal (within 1 to 2 days of the call):** one page covering what is included (pages, number of photos, enquiry method), what is not included, price, timeline, and what you need from him.
2. **Agreement and payment:** confirm the scope in writing and take the advance before starting work.
3. **Collect content:** a shared folder with photos, logo, bio, and a short list of categories and sizes. Set a clear date for this. Your 7-working-day clock should start when you receive the content, not the day he says yes.
4. **Design and structure:** suggested pages are Home, Gallery (by animal, bird or size), Layout ideas (his wall mockups), About, and Enquire/Contact. Keep a calm, premium look, since he wants no noise. The Fraunces and Inter pairing we chose for Uncoded Hub would suit this well.
5. **Build:** compress his photos so the site stays fast. Large wildlife images are the biggest speed risk, and your site promises strong mobile performance.
6. **Review:** one round of changes, agreed in advance, so the project does not drift.
7. **Launch and handover:** domain, hosting, a simple guide for updating photos, and confirmation that he owns everything.
8. **After launch:** check in after one to two weeks, ask for a testimonial, and offer the monthly update plan.

## 8. Watch-outs

- **Scope creep:** if he asks for a shop, a payment gateway, or a booking system for coaching and tours, treat it as a separate paid phase.
- **Photo protection:** his photos already carry a small signature. Ask whether he wants watermarks or download protection, and whether he is comfortable with high-quality images online.
- **Content delays:** most missed deadlines come from late photos and approvals. Set dates for these upfront.
- **Do not promise what you cannot deliver.** If he asks for something outside your skills, say you will check and come back to him.
- **Be honest about fit.** He wants quiet selling, so do not push a big marketing website on him. A simple, elegant gallery matches what he asked for.
