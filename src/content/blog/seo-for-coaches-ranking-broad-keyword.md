*By [Deepak](https://in.linkedin.com/in/deepakdeveloper), co-founder of Uncoded Hub, where he leads sales and strategy. Verified as of September 10, 2026.*

"Business coach" returns millions of results and a market saturated with every possible specialty, price point and credibility level competing on the same generic term. A new coach trying to rank for that word, or anything close to it, is fighting a battle domain age and marketing budget alone will decide. An individual practitioner almost never wins it by competing head-on.

## The short answer

Ranking in this category means defining a market narrow enough to actually dominate, then building genuine topical authority around that narrow definition, rather than competing on the broad, saturated terms the whole industry chases. A specific, well-served niche within coaching ranks far more reliably than "coach" ever will.

## Define the market narrowly enough to actually win it

Peter Thiel's argument in [*Zero to One*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/18050143-zero-to-one), define your market narrowly enough to actually dominate it first and then expand, and don't chase a huge market you can't win outright, is close to a direct SEO strategy applied here. "Business coach" is unwinnable for almost any individual practitioner. A specific niche, such as coaching for first-time startup founders raising a seed round, is a genuinely winnable category, with far less competition and a far more qualified audience already searching in those terms.

## Build silos of topical authority rather than scattered posts

[*Search Engine Optimization All-in-One For Dummies*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/3431311-search-engine-optimization-all-in-one-for-dummies) makes the structural point directly: define your site's single clear theme before picking keywords, and organise your site into silos, meaning clusters of tightly related pages under one theme, cross-linked internally. A coach writing broadly across many loosely related topics never builds the concentrated topical authority a search engine rewards. A coach writing deeply within one clearly defined niche, with every piece cross-linked and reinforcing the same theme, does.

## Compete on differentiation rather than incremental improvement

[*The Blue Ocean Strategy Reader*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/31213452-the-w-chan-kim-and-ren-e-mauborgne-blue-ocean-strategy-reader)'s core argument, stop benchmarking against competitors as your primary reference point because value innovators make competition irrelevant rather than trying to beat it incrementally, is a useful mental shift for a coach trying to rank against an oversaturated category. The goal isn't writing a slightly better generic "how to be a better leader" article than the millions already existing. It's defining and owning a genuinely different, narrower angle with far less content competing for it.

## Own one word, literally

Al Ries' rule from [*The 22 Immutable Laws of Marketing*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/33449.The_22_Immutable_Laws_of_Marketing), own one word in the prospect's mind since a narrow position beats a broad one, is the same principle stated as a positioning rule rather than an SEO tactic, and the two are really the same advice from different angles. The specific positioning covered in [What Makes a Consultant's Website Look Credible vs Templated](/blog/credible-vs-templated-consultant-website) does more than make a site feel credible to a human reader. Mechanically, it's also what makes it rankable at all in a category this saturated.

## What this looks like in practice

A specific, narrow niche statement, stated plainly, that would genuinely sound wrong applied to a different kind of coach. The same specificity test covered in [What Makes a Consultant's Website Look Credible vs Templated](/blog/credible-vs-templated-consultant-website).

Content organised into a genuine cluster, all reinforcing the same narrow theme, cross-linked deliberately rather than scattered across loosely related topics.

Long-tail phrasing throughout, matching the specific way someone in this exact niche actually searches rather than generic industry terms every competitor also chases.

Patience with the timeline. Ryan Holiday's point from [*Perennial Seller*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/32968546-perennial-seller), judge success over years rather than opening-week numbers, applies directly. Ranking within a genuinely narrow, well-served niche still takes real time, even with far less competition than the broad category.

## What to actually do

1. Write down your specific niche, narrow enough to sound wrong applied to a different kind of coach.
2. Audit your existing content for how much actually reinforces that narrow theme versus how much is generic, broadly-applicable advice.
3. Build a genuine content cluster around the narrow niche, cross-linked internally.
4. Use long-tail phrasing matching how your specific audience searches rather than generic industry terms.
5. Give it real time. Narrower ranking is far more achievable than broad ranking without being instant.

## The honest limits of this

Narrowing the focus trades broad reach for genuine rankability. A coach narrowing too far into a niche with too little search volume won't generate meaningful traffic even with zero competition. The goal is narrow enough to be winnable rather than so narrow nobody is searching for it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is it so hard for a coach's website to rank for "coach" or "business coach"?**
These are enormously broad, saturated terms with millions of competing pages and established, high-authority sites already dominating them. An individual coach's site almost never wins that fight through content alone.

**What should a coach target instead of broad category terms?**
A genuinely narrow, specific niche within coaching, defined clearly enough to build real topical authority around. Narrow positioning is both a credibility signal and, mechanically, what makes a site rankable in this saturated category.

**How long does it take to rank within a narrower coaching niche?**
Still real time, even with far less competition. Narrowing the focus makes ranking achievable rather than instant.
