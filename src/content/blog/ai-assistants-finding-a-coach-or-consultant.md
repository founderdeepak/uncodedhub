*By [Geetha](https://www.linkedin.com/in/geethaspecialist), co-founder of Uncoded Hub, where she leads project delivery. Verified as of September 10, 2026.*

Someone types a genuinely detailed question into an AI chat tool: describing their specific situation and a specific problem, then asking what kind of coach they should look for, and whether the tool can suggest anyone. The tool reasons through an answer and, increasingly, names specific people, often citing content it found on their websites. For a category already built on trust and specificity, that's a significant new discovery path.

## The short answer

These tools favour content clearly demonstrating specific expertise and a genuine, well-argued point of view over generic "I help people achieve their potential" positioning. A coach with narrow, specific positioning and real, substantive content is far more likely to be named than one with broad, interchangeable copy that could describe any coach in the category.

## Name the core belief clearly, since an assistant needs something to cite

Russell Brunson's concept of the Big Domino from [*Expert Secrets*](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/35254158-expert-secrets), that every offer needs one false belief which once knocked down makes the sale inevitable, turns out useful for a reason beyond persuasion. A clearly named core belief is exactly the kind of specific, quotable idea an AI assistant can extract and repeat when answering a related question. A homepage with no clear point of view, only generic encouragement, has nothing distinctive for a language model to surface.

## Share genuinely useful content rather than marketing language

Ann Handley's rule from [*Content Rules*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/8102305-content-rules), share or solve rather than shill, describes what these tools are built to extract well. The content-hub material covered in [How a Website Supports the Personal Brand You're Already Building](/blog/website-supports-personal-brand-content), meaning genuine extended arguments rather than marketing copy, is exactly the substantive content giving an AI assistant something real to cite, rather than a vague "transform your life" page with nothing specific in it.

## Own a category specific enough to be confidently named

The narrow-positioning argument covered throughout this cluster, most directly in [What Makes a Consultant's Website Rank When Coach Is a Broad Keyword](/blog/seo-for-coaches-ranking-broad-keyword), matters here in an almost identical way to how it matters for search rankings. An assistant answering a specific question needs to confidently match a specific source to it. A coach with clear, narrow positioning is far easier to name confidently than one with broad, generic copy an assistant would hesitate to recommend for any particular situation.

## Owned content, extended to a new channel

Damian Ryan's framework from [*Understanding Digital Marketing*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/18405805-understanding-digital-marketing), categorise every marketing effort as owned, paid or earned media, applies here the same way as throughout this content plan. AI citation is a new form of earned visibility resting entirely on the quality and specificity of owned website content, rather than something purchasable the way advertising is.

## What actually helps

Clearly named core beliefs and a specific point of view rather than generic encouragement. This is the content most likely to be extracted and repeated accurately.

Genuine, substantive content-hub material, per [How a Website Supports the Personal Brand You're Already Building](/blog/website-supports-personal-brand-content), rather than thin marketing pages.

Narrow, specific positioning, per [What Makes a Consultant's Website Rank When Coach Is a Broad Keyword](/blog/seo-for-coaches-ranking-broad-keyword), helping an assistant confidently attribute an answer to a specific source.

## The honest scope of this, stated fairly

How much coach and consultant discovery already runs through AI tools rather than LinkedIn and traditional search varies significantly and is genuinely hard to state precisely. Any specific adoption figure would be an unfounded guess. What's stable is that the content discipline helping here, covering specificity, genuine substance and a clear point of view, is identical to what already helps LinkedIn conversion and search rankings throughout this cluster.

## What to actually do

1. Name your core belief clearly and specifically rather than as generic encouragement.
2. Build out genuine content-hub material per [How a Website Supports the Personal Brand You're Already Building](/blog/website-supports-personal-brand-content) rather than relying only on marketing copy.
3. Narrow your positioning per [What Makes a Consultant's Website Rank When Coach Is a Broad Keyword](/blog/seo-for-coaches-ranking-broad-keyword), so an assistant can confidently attribute an answer to you specifically.
4. Check your core content isn't locked behind heavy scripting that could make it hard for an automated system to parse.
5. Treat this as a byproduct of the same specificity discipline covered throughout this cluster rather than a separate project.

## The honest limits of this

No specific tactic guarantees citation by any particular AI tool, and this channel's behaviour is still evolving quickly. The safest investment remains the one helping everywhere else in this cluster: genuine, specific, substantive content, which performs better here for the same underlying reasons.

## Frequently asked questions

**How are AI assistants changing how people find a coach or consultant?**
Some people now ask AI chat tools directly for guidance on their specific situation, and these tools tend to cite coaches with clear, specific positioning and genuine, substantive content over generic, interchangeable marketing copy.

**What makes a coach's website more likely to be cited by an AI assistant?**
A clearly named core belief, genuine content-hub material with real substance, and narrow positioning specific enough for an assistant to confidently attribute an answer to.

**Should a coach build a separate strategy for AI search specifically?**
No. The same specificity and content discipline covered throughout this cluster helps here too, so it's a byproduct of doing that well rather than a separate initiative.
