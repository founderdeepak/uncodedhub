Ask ten interior designers whether they should put prices on their website, and nine will tell you: *"Never. Every project is unique. If I list a price, it will scare people away before I can explain our value on a call."*

Then ask those same nine designers what their biggest frustration is.

Without fail, they will tell you: *"I spend half my week on the phone with people who love our work but have an unrealistic budget of five lakhs for a four-bedroom home."*

The fear of publishing prices creates the exact problem designers complain about most. When you refuse to give any price signal online, you force everyone to guess. And when people guess, they assume your prices are whatever they hope they are.

## The short answer

You should never publish rigid menu pricing for custom interior design because site conditions, civil variations, and material selections vary widely. However, you should absolutely publish your minimum engagement threshold and typical project brackets (for example, *"Turnkey engagements typically range between ₹25L and ₹60L, with a minimum project commitment of ₹20L"*). This disarms client anxiety, eliminates low-budget inquiries, and positions your firm as an established, transparent authority.

## Why hiding prices completely creates friction for affluent buyers

In [*Making Websites Win*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/39668820-making-websites-win), Karl Blanks demonstrates that when buyers encounter missing pricing information on a high-value service site, they do not think: *"I will call them to find out."*

They think: *"If they don't say what they charge, either they are outrageously expensive, or they make up quotes depending on how wealthy I look."*

Lack of pricing clarity reads as a lack of transparency.

In [*Building a StoryBrand*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/34460583-building-a-storybrand), Donald Miller reminds us that customers want clarity above all else. When an executive or business owner browses your portfolio in the evening, they want to know one simple thing: *Is this studio in my league?*

If they are planning a fifty-lakh interior and your site looks like an amateur student project with no pricing guidance, they assume you cannot handle their scale. If your site looks like a museum with zero pricing signals, they assume you only work for Bollywood celebrities and move on to the next tab.

## The 3 ways to present design pricing responsibly

You do not need to publish a rigid per-sq-ft price list. In fact, doing so is dangerous because per-sq-ft rates ignore site complexity and encourage transactional bargaining.

Instead, use one of three tested positioning models:

### 1. The Minimum Project Commitment (The Cleanest Filter)
This is the simplest and most effective model for established studios:
> *"We take on a limited number of turnkey residential projects each season. Our minimum project commitment is ₹25 Lakhs."*

This single sentence instantly filters out fifty percent of time-wasting inquiries. Homeowners who have twenty-five lakhs or more appreciate the exclusivity; homeowners who do not are spared a disappointing introductory call.

### 2. Typical Investment Brackets by Flat Configuration
Homeowners think in terms of their property type (3BHK, 4BHK, Villa). Providing historical ranges helps them anchor expectations:
- 3BHK Turnkey Interior: Typically ₹22L – ₹35L (depending on veneer, hardware, and civil scope)
- 4BHK & Penthouse: Typically ₹35L – ₹65L
- Bespoke Private Villa: Typically ₹60L – ₹1.2 Cr+

Follow this bracket with an honest note explaining what drives variance: *"The primary drivers of project investment are civil modifications, premium kitchen hardware (Blum/Hafele), centralized automation, and custom millwork finishes."*

### 3. The Percentage Fee Structure (For Pure Architectural Design)
If you provide architectural consultation and supervision rather than turnkey execution, explain your fee calculation model clearly:
> *"Our architectural design fee is structured as 10% to 12% of total civil and interior execution cost, with staged milestone payments linked to site deliverables."*

## What happens to your conversion rate when you publish pricing

As we analyzed in our study on [[03 - How Much Should an Interior Design Studio Budget for a Website]], studios that publish clear financial parameters see an immediate thirty to forty percent drop in total form submissions.

At first glance, that sounds alarming.

However, their consultation-to-contract conversion rate doubles. Instead of taking ten introductory calls to sign one project, they take three calls and sign two. Every homeowner who books a consultation is already pre-sold on their budget framework.

When you respect your client's time enough to be honest about money, they respect your design authority enough to pay your fees.
