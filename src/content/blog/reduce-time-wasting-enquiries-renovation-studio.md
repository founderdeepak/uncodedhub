*By [Deepak](https://in.linkedin.com/in/deepakdeveloper), co-founder of Uncoded Hub, where he leads sales and strategy. Verified as of September 10, 2026.*

Most time-wasting enquiries aren't a personality mismatch or bad luck. They're the predictable result of a website that didn't answer a question the homeowner needed answered before messaging. The information gap doesn't disappear when the enquiry comes in. It gets pushed into a call, where it takes far longer to resolve than a sentence on the page would have.

## The short answer

The enquiries wasting the most time are usually missing the same handful of things upfront. A price range, a clear service area, an honest minimum project size, and a described process. None of these are complicated to add. They're skipped because "every project is different" gets used as a reason to say nothing rather than a reason to say something useful.

## Pre-empt the questions rather than letting the call surface them

Ryan Deiss and Russ Henneberry's rule from [*Digital Marketing For Dummies*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/33645930-digital-marketing-for-dummies), map your customer's objections explicitly and address each one directly rather than waiting for them to surface in sales calls, is the discipline reducing this specific problem. A homeowner who has to call just to find out your minimum project size, your service area or a rough price range isn't a bad-fit lead. They're asking because the site made them ask. Every one of those questions answered on the page is a call that either doesn't happen at all, because the answer disqualified them, which is a good outcome, or arrives already past that stage, which is a better one.

## Solve rather than only showcase

Ann Handley's principle from [*Content Rules*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/8102305-content-rules), share or solve rather than shill, is a useful diagnostic for a renovation site's existing content. A page of only finished-project photos showcases. A page also answering "what's your minimum kitchen size", "do you work in [specific area]" and "what's a realistic budget for a 2BHK" solves. Solving is what filters an enquiry before it's sent rather than after.

## What specifically causes time-wasting enquiries

No stated minimum project size or scope. A homeowner looking for a single wardrobe unit and a studio specialising in full-home renovations both end up in the same enquiry pipeline if neither side states what they take on.

No service area stated clearly. An enquiry from outside your working radius costs a full back-and-forth just to establish it isn't going anywhere.

No price range at all. This is the biggest driver of misaligned enquiries in this niche. As covered in [What a Modular Kitchen & Renovation Website Should Include](/blog/what-a-modular-kitchen-renovation-website-should-include), silence doesn't protect against price-shopping. It means every enquiry starts from zero information on both sides.

No described process. Without one, a share of enquiries are really just questions about how the whole thing works, which a written process page would have answered without a call.

## The filtering effect, and why it's a feature

Al Ries' logic from [*The 22 Immutable Laws of Marketing*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/33449.The_22_Immutable_Laws_of_Marketing), own one word since a narrow position beats a broad one, applies here too. Stating your actual scope narrowly, covering minimum project size, specific service area and a defined starting price, informs and filters at the same time. A homeowner reading a clearly stated minimum they don't fit self-selects out before messaging. That looks like fewer enquiries on paper and means fewer *wasted* hours on both sides.

## What to actually add

1. State your minimum project size or scope clearly, if one genuinely exists.
2. Name your actual service area explicitly rather than leaving it implied by your business address.
3. Add a starting price range, even a soft one. This single change does more filtering than any other item on this list.
4. Write out your process in plain terms, so "how does this work" enquiries get answered before they're asked.
5. Review your last ten enquiries and note which would have been avoided, or arrived further along, if this information had already been on the page.

## The honest limits of this

Filtering reduces time-wasting enquiries without eliminating every mismatch, since a homeowner can still misread stated information or hope an exception applies. It also depends on the stated details being accurate. A minimum project size or price range that doesn't match reality shifts the friction to a later, more frustrating point in the conversation rather than removing it.

## Frequently asked questions

**What causes most time-wasting enquiries for a renovation business?**
Missing information forcing a homeowner to call just to find out basics: minimum project size, service area, rough price range, and how the process works.

**Does stating a price range reduce total enquiries?**
Usually yes, and that's the intended effect. It filters out budget-mismatched enquiries before they're sent, reducing volume while improving the average quality and readiness of the enquiries remaining.

**Is it better to say nothing about pricing than to give a range that might not fit every project?**
No. Silence doesn't protect against price mismatches. It means every enquiry starts from zero shared information, which is what causes the longest, most time-wasting calls.
