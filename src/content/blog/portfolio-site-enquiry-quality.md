*By [Geetha](https://www.linkedin.com/in/geethaspecialist), co-founder of Uncoded Hub, where she leads project delivery. Verified as of September 10, 2026.*

Most studios measure a website redesign by whether enquiries went up. That's the easy number to track and often the wrong one to optimise for. A redesign can genuinely increase enquiry count while making the average enquiry worse: more tire-kickers, more budget mismatches, more people who never respond to the follow-up.

The more useful question is whether the *right* enquiries went up.

## The short answer

A faster, better-structured portfolio site changes who reaches out and how prepared they are when they do, because clearer positioning and real project context filter for fit before the message is sent. Volume and quality can move in opposite directions, and measuring only volume hides that.

## Why volume alone is the wrong metric

Damian Ryan's principle from [*Understanding Digital Marketing*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/18405805-understanding-digital-marketing) applies directly to how a redesign should be judged: choose KPIs deliberately tied to business goals rather than vanity metrics like raw traffic. Enquiry count is a vanity metric unless it's paired with what happened next. How many of those enquiries had a real budget. How many turned into a booked consultation. How many became a signed project. A studio chasing raw enquiry volume can improve the wrong number while closed projects stay flat, or even drop as the sales side drowns in unqualified messages.

## What structure does to enquiry quality

Karl Blanks' instruction in [*Making Websites Win*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/39668820-making-websites-win) explains the mechanism: make benefits explicit and concrete, because visitors won't infer value and you have to state it directly. A vague site forces every visitor to guess whether they're a fit, so people self-select in almost randomly. A specific site, with clear positioning and real project scope and budget bands visible in the case studies, lets visitors self-select accurately before they message. The unqualified visitor sees a project value band well above their budget and doesn't bother. The qualified one sees exactly what they were looking for and arrives already primed.

## Speed's effect on who finishes the journey

A slow site doesn't reduce enquiries evenly across every kind of visitor. It disproportionately loses the impatient, comparison-shopping visitor checking five studios in one sitting, who won't wait for the slow one to load.

There's a trap hidden in that. A slow site's *remaining* enquiries can look artificially high-quality on average, purely because only the most patient, already-committed visitors stuck around. It looks like quality improved, when in fact a whole segment of viable prospects silently dropped off before reaching the contact form.

## The objection-mapping effect

Ryan Deiss and Russ Henneberry's principle from [*Digital Marketing For Dummies*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/33645930-digital-marketing-for-dummies) describes what a well-structured case study does that a bare gallery can't: map your customer's objections explicitly and address each one directly in your copy, rather than waiting for them to surface in sales calls. A visitor with real budget and real intent usually has two or three specific worries. Can this studio handle a project my size. Will the timeline actually hold. Is this within my range. Content that answers those before the enquiry is sent means the person who does message has already resolved their major hesitations, which is a measurably more prepared enquiry than a cold "please share details".

## How to measure this honestly

Track two things side by side after any change to the site, alongside enquiry count. The percentage of enquiries that state a real project scope or budget unprompted, and the percentage that convert to a booked consultation. If enquiry count rises but both of those fall, the change made the site more attractive to the wrong audience, which is a useful signal a raw count alone would hide completely.

## What to actually do

1. Start tracking enquiry quality alongside count. Did the message include real scope or budget context, unprompted?
2. Add real project scope to your case studies, per [What an Interior Designer's Website Should Include](/blog/what-an-interior-designers-website-should-include), so visitors self-select before messaging.
3. Fix load speed, then watch whether enquiry count and quality move together or apart. That tells you whether your prior "quality" was really just survivorship among patient visitors.
4. Pre-empt the two or three objections a qualified visitor most likely has, directly in your case study copy.
5. Review this monthly rather than only after a redesign, since enquiry quality drifts over time even without site changes.

## The honest limits of this

A better-structured site filters and primes. It doesn't manufacture interest that isn't there in your service area, and it can't turn a genuinely weak portfolio into a strong one. It changes who reaches out among the people already going to consider studios like yours, without expanding that pool on its own.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does a faster, better-structured website really change enquiry quality rather than just volume?**
Yes. Clearer positioning and real project context let visitors self-select for fit before messaging, and speed changes who's patient enough to complete that journey at all. The two effects can move enquiry count and enquiry quality in different directions.

**How do you measure enquiry quality instead of just volume?**
Track whether enquiries include real scope or budget context unprompted, and what percentage convert to a booked consultation, alongside the raw number of messages received.

**Can a slow website ever look like it has higher enquiry quality?**
Yes, misleadingly. If only the most patient, already-committed visitors stick around to enquire, the remaining sample looks artificially high-quality while a whole segment of viable prospects drops off before reaching the contact form.
