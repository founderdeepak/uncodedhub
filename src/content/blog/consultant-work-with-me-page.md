*By [Geetha](https://www.linkedin.com/in/geethaspecialist), co-founder of Uncoded Hub, where she leads project delivery. Verified as of September 10, 2026.*

By the time a visitor reaches a Work With Me page, they aren't being introduced to the practice. They're deciding whether to act. Most of these pages don't seem to know that. They repeat the homepage's positioning, list a few services, and end with a generic contact form, treating the most decision-ready visitor on the entire site the same as someone who just landed for the first time.

## The short answer

A Work With Me page should assume the visitor already believes the underlying idea and needs exactly three things to act. A clear description of what happens when they work with you. A specific answer to their remaining hesitations. And one obvious next step. Repeating the homepage's introductory pitch here wastes the page's real job.

## The transition is a distinct skill

Jason Fladlien's structure from [*One to Many*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/40405984-one-to-many), a pitch has four parts in strict order of introduction, content, transition and close, and the transition is a distinct skill of bridging from free content to the offer without a jarring, salesy tone shift, maps directly onto what a Work With Me page is. It's the transition and close, arriving after a visitor has already consumed the introduction and content elsewhere on the site. A page suddenly shifting into a hard, generic sales register here, after everything before it felt genuine and specific, creates exactly the jarring tone break Fladlien warns against. The close should feel like a natural continuation of the value already given rather than a separate pivot into selling mode.

## Give a plan rather than a description

Donald Miller's rule from [*Building a StoryBrand*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/34460583-building-a-storybrand), give the customer a clear, simple plan of three steps or fewer so the path forward feels safe rather than risky, is the structural core of a good Work With Me page. Not a service description written in the abstract, but the literal sequence. What happens after they book a call. What the engagement looks like week to week. How it concludes. Uncertainty about mechanics is one of the quieter reasons a warm, ready visitor still hesitates at this exact page.

## Answer the objection this specific visitor still has

Ryan Deiss and Russ Henneberry's principle from [*Digital Marketing For Dummies*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/33645930-digital-marketing-for-dummies), map your customer's objections explicitly and address each one directly rather than waiting for them to surface in sales calls, matters more here than almost anywhere else on the site. A visitor who's made it this far has usually resolved their broad doubts and is stuck on one or two specific, practical ones. Cost. Time commitment. Whether this applies to their exact situation. Naming and answering those here, rather than saving them for the discovery call, removes the last real barrier to booking.

## What belongs on this page

A specific, sequenced description of the engagement. Not a vague service category, but what actually happens, in order, once someone starts working with you.

Direct answers to the two or three most common remaining hesitations at this stage, covering cost range, time commitment, and whether it applies to a specific kind of situation.

Specific, outcome-based proof per [What Makes a Consultant's Website Look Credible vs Templated](/blog/credible-vs-templated-consultant-website), placed here rather than only on a general testimonials page. This is the moment it does the most work.

One clear, singular call to action with nothing competing for attention. "Book a discovery call", repeated, rather than buried among several equally weighted options.

## What to avoid

Repeating the homepage's introductory positioning pitch wastes the page's real function. A visitor reaching this page already believes the premise, so restating it reads as padding rather than progress. Equally, ending on a generic contact form with no context, just name, email and message, fails to capitalise on a visitor ready to act on something specific right now.

## What to actually build

1. Rewrite the page to assume the visitor already believes your premise, cutting any content re-explaining the homepage's core positioning.
2. Write out the actual sequence of the engagement, step by step, rather than a vague service description.
3. Directly name and answer the two or three most common remaining hesitations at this stage.
4. Add specific, outcome-based proof placed on this exact page rather than only linked elsewhere.
5. Make the call to action singular and unmissable, with nothing else competing for the visitor's attention.

## The honest limits of this

A well-structured page converts a genuinely ready visitor more reliably. It can't manufacture readiness in someone who isn't there yet, and pushing too hard for commitment from a visitor still resolving broader doubts just pushes them back to earlier content, or away entirely. This page's job is closing an already-warm decision rather than starting one.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should a consultant's "Work With Me" page actually say?**
A specific, sequenced description of what happens when someone works with you, direct answers to the remaining hesitations a ready visitor has, specific outcome-based proof, and one clear, singular call to action.

**Why shouldn't this page repeat the homepage's introductory pitch?**
A visitor reaching this page already believes the underlying premise, so restating it wastes the page's real job of closing a decision rather than introducing an idea again.
