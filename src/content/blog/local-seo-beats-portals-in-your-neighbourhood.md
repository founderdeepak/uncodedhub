*By [Deepak](https://in.linkedin.com/in/deepakdeveloper), co-founder of Uncoded Hub, where he leads sales and strategy. Verified as of September 10, 2026.*

99acres and MagicBricks will always outrank an individual agent for a broad search like "flats for sale Bengaluru". What they can't easily do is outrank a genuinely specific, well-built page about one particular micro-market, because a portal's content about that area is thin by design, aggregated across thousands of listings, while an agent who actually works that neighbourhood can write something a portal structurally can't.

## The short answer

Big portals win broad searches through sheer domain authority and volume. They lose narrow, hyperlocal searches to a genuinely specific, well-written page about one neighbourhood, because that specificity is exactly what a portal's aggregated listing format can't replicate. Local SEO for an individual agent means competing on narrowness rather than scale.

## Reverse the demand-generation formula

Mark Roberge's core argument in [*The Sales Acceleration Formula*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/24953002-the-sales-acceleration-formula) is to reverse the demand-generation formula, getting buyers to find you through content and SEO instead of chasing them. That's the mechanism at play. An agent competing head-on with a portal for broad reach is chasing a fight they can't win with resources they don't have. An agent building narrow, genuinely useful local content instead lets the buyer who's already searching specifically for their neighbourhood find them, on a page a portal hasn't bothered to write.

## Own the word a portal can't own

Al Ries' rule from [*The 22 Immutable Laws of Marketing*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/33449.The_22_Immutable_Laws_of_Marketing), own one word in the prospect's mind since a narrow position beats a broad one, explains why this works structurally. A portal owns "property listings [city]", an enormous, generic category. It doesn't and structurally can't own resale price trends in one specific society or micro-market, because that requires granular, ongoing local knowledge no aggregator is built to produce at scale. That category is entirely open for an individual agent to claim.

## What content actually wins this fight

Genuinely written neighbourhood pages carry real price trends, real inventory notes, and real commute and infrastructure detail specific to one area, rather than a template repeated across twenty locations. This is the exact content type a portal's aggregated listing pages can't match.

Answers to the specific questions buyers in that area actually ask work the same way. What's the typical resale premium in a specific society. What's changed in the area in the last year. Which blocks have better appreciation history. A portal's generic city-wide content has no equivalent.

Consistent, category-specific presence matters too. A Google Business Profile categorised and described around the same narrow positioning, rather than a generic "real estate agent" listing competing on the broadest possible terms.

## Owned versus rented visibility

Damian Ryan's distinction in [*Understanding Digital Marketing*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/18405805-understanding-digital-marketing) is to categorise every marketing effort as owned, paid or earned media, and track each differently. Applied to the portal-versus-website question, a portal listing is closer to rented media. Visible only while you keep paying or keep the listing active, always inside someone else's interface, always competing against other agents on the same page. A well-built local page is owned media. It keeps ranking and keeps working without ongoing payment, and you control it fully rather than having its layout shaped by a portal's decisions.

## Why this is realistic rather than aspirational

To be clear about the honest scope, this doesn't mean outranking portals for broad city-wide terms. That fight genuinely isn't winnable for most individual agents. It means winning the much narrower, much higher-intent searches that happen inside your specific working area, where a portal's breadth is a disadvantage rather than a strength.

## What to actually build

1. Pick two or three micro-markets you genuinely know well and commit to owning their local search, rather than covering the whole city thinly.
2. Write one genuinely detailed page per micro-market with real price trends and inventory notes, not a template.
3. Answer the specific questions buyers in that area ask, based on what you're already asked on real calls.
4. Align your Google Business Profile category and description with the same narrow positioning.
5. Track rankings for these specific local terms rather than broad city-wide ones you were never going to win against a portal.

## The honest limits of this

This is a slower path than paying for portal visibility, and it only works for micro-markets you can genuinely write about with real, current knowledge. A thin page about an area you don't actually know fails the same test a portal's generic content does, just on a smaller scale. It's also not a replacement for portal presence, which still captures buyers who haven't narrowed down to a specific area.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can an individual real estate agent really outrank 99acres or MagicBricks?**
Not for broad city-wide searches. For narrow, hyperlocal searches about a specific neighbourhood or society, a genuinely detailed page can outrank a portal's thin, aggregated content for that same area.

**What kind of content actually competes with property portals locally?**
Genuinely written neighbourhood pages with real price trends and local detail, which is the specific, granular content a portal's aggregated listing format structurally can't produce.

**Should an agent stop advertising on portals if their local SEO is working?**
No. Portals still capture buyers who haven't narrowed their search to a specific area yet. Local SEO wins a different, narrower part of the buyer's journey.
