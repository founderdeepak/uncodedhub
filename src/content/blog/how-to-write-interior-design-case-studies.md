# How to Write Interior Design Case Studies That Sell Premium Projects

*By [Geetha](https://www.linkedin.com/in/geethaspecialist), co-founder of Uncoded Hub, where she leads project delivery. Verified as of September 26, 2026.*

A gallery of twelve immaculate photographs is not a case study. It is a photo album.

When an affluent homeowner visits an interior designer's website, they already know you can take pretty pictures. What they cannot tell from pictures alone is whether you are easy to work with, whether you stay on budget, how you handle civil defects from the builder, and whether you understand how real families live.

If your project pages contain only an image grid and a two-sentence caption saying *"A serene modern 4BHK in Indiranagar finished in warm neutrals"*, you are forcing the client to judge you purely on visual taste. Visual taste is subjective and easily copied on Pinterest. Problem-solving capability is what commands a fifty-lakh fee.

## The short answer

A high-converting interior design case study follows a four-part narrative structure: the client's original lifestyle brief, the architectural and spatial constraints of the site, the custom material and engineering interventions implemented, and the measurable outcome. Explaining why you tore down a kitchen partition or how you solved poor cross-ventilation proves your technical authority before the first consultation.

## The StoryBrand framework for project stories

In [*Building a StoryBrand*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/34460583-building-a-storybrand), Donald Miller emphasizes that customers look for a guide who possesses two traits: empathy and authority.

Most design portfolios demonstrate neither. They showcase the designer as the hero (*"Look at this brilliant marble island we selected"*).

A real case study positions the client as the hero who faced a concrete problem, with your firm acting as the experienced guide who steered them past pitfalls:
1. The Hero's Dilemma: A young couple with two dogs and elderly parents moving into a concrete shell with dark corridors and rigid structural columns.
2. The Guide's Insight: Identifying that standard builder layouts compartmentalize light, and proposing concealed pocket doors and acoustic insulation.
3. The Execution: Sourcing durable quartz instead of porous Italian marble to withstand heavy Indian cooking, paired with concealed ducted air conditioning.
4. The Resolution: A home delivered two weeks ahead of Diwali where three generations live comfortably without acoustic interference.

## The 4-part anatomy of a compelling project page

Every case study on your site should follow a consistent, scannable format.

### 1. The Project Metadata Box (The Facts Up Front)
Busy prospective clients want context before reading prose. Place an editorial facts bar directly below the title:
- Location: Indiranagar, Bengaluru
- Property: 3,400 sq ft, 4BHK Penthouse
- Scope: Full Turnkey Interior Architecture & Bespoke Millwork
- Timeline: 14 Weeks from Demolition to Handover
- Family Profile: Tech executive couple with two school-age children

### 2. The Client's Brief and Lifestyle Friction
Describe what wasn't working in the builder's original handover. 
Did the builder place the utility area in a dark corner? Was the master bedroom missing adequate wardrobe depth? Did the family need an acoustically separated study for late-night international video calls? Documenting the brief shows future clients that you listen to requirements instead of imposing a signature style onto everyone.

### 3. The Engineering & Design Solutions
This is where you demonstrate architectural depth. Instead of saying *"we used warm oak veneer"*, explain the technical rationale:
- *"Because the living room faces west and receives intense afternoon heat, we avoided open-grain veneers on window surrounds and installed motorized solar-reflective blinds concealed inside ceiling coves."*
- *"To preserve ceiling height while running centralized ventilation, we designed multi-tiered drywall drops that align with the kitchen cabinetry lines."*

This level of explanation reassures clients that you understand structural building dynamics, not just decorative styling.

### 4. Client Reflection & Handover Proof
End the case study with direct client commentary. Not a vague *"They did a great job!"*, but specific praise on process:
*"Geetha and her team gave us a detailed Gantt chart on Day One. When our Italian tiles were delayed at the port, they shifted carpentry forward so the overall handover date didn't slip by a single day."*

## Balancing images with scannable text

Do not bury your photographs under thousands of words of academic architectural critique. As we discussed in [[09 - What Makes a Good Architecture Portfolio Website (Deeper Look)]], photography carries the initial emotional hook, while narrative text closes the credibility gap.

Alternate between:
- Full-width hero transformation shots
- Side-by-side split screens showing the raw concrete site vs the finished millwork
- Micro-captions under detail shots explaining hardware choices (such as Blum tip-on mechanisms or concealed Hafele hinges)

When your case studies read like forensic breakdowns of successful projects, price-shoppers disappear. What remains are clients who want that exact level of engineering precision for their own home.
