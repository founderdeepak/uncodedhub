*By [Deepak](https://in.linkedin.com/in/deepakdeveloper), co-founder of Uncoded Hub, where he leads sales and strategy. Verified as of September 10, 2026.*

A buyer types something into an AI chat tool that no portal search box could handle: "I'm looking at resale flats in a specific area, what should I know before choosing an agent, and are there any I should look at specifically?" The tool answers with reasoning and, increasingly, with names, often citing content it found on an agent's own site. This is a new entry point into a buyer's decision, sitting alongside portals and Google search rather than replacing either.

## The short answer

These tools tend to cite specific, genuinely useful content over generic marketing pages. An agent with real neighbourhood-specific insight, a clearly stated fee structure and well-structured content is more likely to be named than one with a thin, listings-only site. The response is the same content discipline that helps local SEO and buyer trust everywhere else, rather than a separate strategy.

## Owned content, extended to a new channel

Damian Ryan's framework from [*Understanding Digital Marketing*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/18405805-understanding-digital-marketing), categorise every marketing effort as owned, paid or earned media and track each differently, is worth extending to this newer channel. Being surfaced by an AI assistant is a form of earned visibility built entirely on owned content. It can't be purchased the way a portal listing can, and it depends on the same genuinely useful website content covered in [What Makes a Buyer Trust a Property Consultant's Website](/blog/what-makes-a-buyer-trust-a-property-consultants-website).

## Share genuinely useful content rather than marketing copy

Ann Handley's rule from [*Content Rules*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/8102305-content-rules), share or solve rather than shill, describes what these tools are built to extract and repeat. A page that genuinely explains what buyers should check before purchasing resale in a specific area, or how price trends have moved in a specific micro-market, is content an AI assistant can use to answer a real question. A page that only says "trusted agent, excellent service" has nothing extractable in it.

## Own a category specific enough to be confidently cited

Al Ries' rule from [*The 22 Immutable Laws of Marketing*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/33449.The_22_Immutable_Laws_of_Marketing), own one word in the prospect's mind since a narrow position beats a broad one, applies directly. An assistant answering a specific question needs to confidently attribute an answer to a specific source. An agent with a clear, narrow specialty, whether a specific micro-market or a specific buyer type, is easier to cite accurately than one with broad, undifferentiated positioning.

## What actually helps

Genuine local content, meaning the neighbourhood pages and price-trend insight covered in [How Local SEO Beats the Big Portals in Your Own Neighbourhood](/blog/local-seo-beats-portals-in-your-neighbourhood). This is exactly the specific, extractable content these tools favour.

A plainly stated fee structure and process, answering a real buyer question directly rather than requiring a call to find out.

Structured, clean page content, free of heavy script-dependent presentation that makes content hard for any automated system to reliably parse.

## The honest scope of this, stated fairly

It's genuinely hard to know how much current buyer research already runs through AI tools rather than portals and search. It varies by buyer, it's evolving fast, and any specific adoption figure would be a guess dressed as a fact. What's stable is that the underlying content discipline, meaning specific, genuinely useful and well-structured, is the same discipline that already helps with search rankings and buyer trust. There's no separate investment required to also benefit here.

## What to actually do

1. Make sure your fee structure and process are stated plainly somewhere on your site rather than gated behind a call.
2. Write genuinely specific local content of the kind covered elsewhere in this cluster, rather than generic city-wide copy.
3. Check your site's core content isn't locked behind heavy scripting that could make it hard for an automated tool to parse.
4. Treat this as a natural extension of good local SEO and content practice rather than a separate initiative.

## The honest limits of this

No specific tactic guarantees citation by any particular AI tool, and behaviour here is still evolving. The safest bet remains the one that helps everywhere else in this content plan: genuinely useful, specific, honest content outperforms generic marketing copy, regardless of which channel eventually surfaces it.

## Frequently asked questions

**How are AI assistants changing how property buyers find an agent?**
Some buyers now ask conversational AI tools directly for guidance and recommendations alongside using portals and Google, and these tools tend to cite specific, genuinely useful content over generic marketing pages.

**What makes an agent's website more likely to be cited by an AI assistant?**
Genuine local content, a plainly stated fee structure, and clean, well-structured pages. The same qualities that help traditional local SEO and buyer trust.

**Should an agent build a separate strategy for AI search specifically?**
No. The practices that help here overlap almost entirely with existing good local SEO and content discipline, so it's a byproduct of doing that well rather than a separate project.
