*By [Geetha](https://www.linkedin.com/in/geethaspecialist), co-founder of Uncoded Hub, where she leads project delivery. Verified as of September 10, 2026.*

"SEO included" appears on almost every web design quote in this category, and it means wildly different things from one studio to the next. Sometimes a genuine, defined set of technical work. Sometimes little more than the phrase itself with nothing concrete behind it. A business owner comparing quotes has almost no way to tell the difference without asking directly, because the phrase costs a studio nothing to include either way.

## The short answer

"SEO included" should mean a specific, checkable set of technical and structural work rather than a vague promise. At minimum: a defined site theme with researched keywords, clean URL and permalink structure, technical fundamentals like page speed and mobile responsiveness, and basic structured data. Anything less is marketing language wearing an SEO label.

## Define the theme before anything else

[*Search Engine Optimization All-in-One For Dummies*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/3431311-search-engine-optimization-all-in-one-for-dummies) states the starting point plainly: define your site's single clear theme first, covering what it is and explicitly what it is not, before picking any keywords, since a diluted, unfocused theme confuses search engines and users alike. This is real strategic work that has to happen before any technical SEO checklist matters. A site with excellent technical SEO and no clear theme still underperforms, because the foundational clarity isn't there.

## The technical fundamentals that count

[*WordPress SEO*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/25028885-wordpress-seo)'s concrete list is a useful baseline regardless of platform. Eliminate duplicate content. Set non-essential pages to noindex. Control title tags and meta descriptions per page rather than relying on defaults. Optimise page load speed. Build clean, keyword-relevant permalinks, internal links and image alt text as standard practice on every page. Every one of these is checkable, so a business owner can look at their own site and verify whether each item is true rather than taking a studio's word for it.

## Organise into silos rather than scattered pages

The same source adds a structural point worth including in any real definition of included: organise your site into silos, meaning clusters of tightly related pages under one theme, cross-linked internally, so search engines can clearly see topical relevance. A site with individually optimised pages and no deliberate internal linking structure between related content is doing SEO in pieces rather than as a coherent system.

## Track the right outcome rather than a vanity metric

Damian Ryan's principle from [*Understanding Digital Marketing*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/18405805-understanding-digital-marketing), choose KPIs deliberately tied to business goals rather than vanity metrics like raw traffic, is worth applying to how "SEO included" gets judged after launch. A studio should be able to explain what was done and why, tied to real business goals, rather than pointing at a traffic number with no context for whether that traffic is the right audience.

## What "SEO included" should concretely cover

Keyword research and a defined site theme, done before design begins rather than retrofitted afterward.

Clean technical fundamentals, covering proper title tags and meta descriptions per page, correct permalink structure, mobile responsiveness and page speed optimisation, each checkable individually.

Basic structured data, meaning schema markup relevant to the business type, a sitemap and correct indexing settings.

A deliberate internal linking structure rather than only individually optimised standalone pages.

A clear explanation of what's not included. Ongoing content creation, link building and continued optimisation are typically separate, ongoing work rather than a one-time inclusion, and a studio should say so plainly rather than letting "SEO included" imply more than the initial build covers.

## What to ask before accepting the phrase at face value

1. Ask for the specific list of what "SEO included" covers, item by item, rather than only the phrase.
2. Ask who does the keyword research and site theme definition, and when in the process it happens.
3. Ask what's explicitly not included, since ongoing SEO work is usually separate and a studio should be upfront about that boundary.
4. Check the technical fundamentals yourself once the site is live. Most of the checklist above is independently verifiable without technical expertise.
5. Ask what KPI the studio would use to judge whether the SEO work succeeded, and whether it's tied to your actual business goals or only raw traffic.

## The honest limits of this

Even a genuinely comprehensive "SEO included" package covers foundational technical and structural work. It doesn't replace the ongoing content creation and authority-building mattering long after launch, covered throughout the niche clusters in this content plan. A one-time inclusion sets the foundation rather than substituting for continued effort.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should "website design with SEO included" actually mean?**
A specific, checkable set of technical and structural work covering keyword research and a defined site theme, clean technical fundamentals, basic structured data and deliberate internal linking, rather than only the phrase with nothing concrete behind it.

**How can a business owner verify SEO was actually included rather than only claimed?**
Most of the technical fundamentals, covering title tags, permalink structure, mobile responsiveness and structured data, are independently checkable on a live site without technical expertise.

**Does "SEO included" cover ongoing search ranking work after launch?**
Usually not. A one-time inclusion typically covers the foundational technical and structural setup, while ongoing content creation and authority-building is separate, continuing work a studio should state plainly.
