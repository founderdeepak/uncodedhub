# The Interior Design Client Questionnaire: How to Filter Inquiries on Your Website

*By [Deepak](https://in.linkedin.com/in/deepakdeveloper), co-founder of Uncoded Hub, where he leads sales and strategy. Verified as of September 26, 2026.*

Every studio owner knows the feeling of opening an inquiry email that says: *"Hi, please share your catalogue and price list."*

You do not have a catalogue. You design bespoke residential spaces. Answering that email takes twenty minutes of typing, a polite explanation that every project is custom, and an invitation to get on a call. Nine times out of ten, the prospect never replies. They wanted a 2BHK modular package for four lakhs, and you do not take projects below twenty-five.

The problem is not the prospect. The problem is a contact form that treated a bespoke architectural commitment like a newsletter signup.

## The short answer

A four-field contact form (Name, Email, Phone, Message) attracts low-commitment inquiries because it asks for zero thought. Replacing it with a structured five-step inquiry questionnaire forces the prospect to declare their possession date, floor plan type, scope, and realistic budget range before they hit submit. Serious homeowners appreciate the thoroughness because it mirrors the attention to detail they expect from a designer. Price-shoppers drop off immediately, protecting your design team's time.

## Why generic contact forms sabotage design studios

Donald Miller writes in [*Building a StoryBrand*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/34460583-building-a-storybrand) that customers do not buy the best products; they buy the products they can understand the fastest. When an interior design site displays a vague "Let's Talk" button leading to an empty text box, the customer has no framework for what information you actually need.

They write what feels natural: *"Need interior design for my flat. How much do you charge per square foot?"*

Now you are trapped in a price-first conversation before you have even established what they want to build.

In [*Making Websites Win*](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/39668820-making-websites-win), Karl Blanks notes that friction in web forms is not inherently bad. While e-commerce checkouts require minimal friction to maximize micro-transactions, high-ticket consultative services require qualifying friction. Qualifying friction repels people who cannot afford you while deepening commitment from clients who can.

## The 5 questions every interior design questionnaire must ask

A consultation form should take between ninety seconds and two minutes to complete. It should never feel like a government tax audit, but it must gather five critical data points.

### 1. Project type and square footage
Do not ask for open-ended descriptions. Give clear selector buttons:
- Full Home Turnkey (New Handover)
- Renovation / Remodel
- Penthouse / Villa
- Commercial / Office Space

Follow this with an approximate carpet area selector: Under 1,500 sq ft, 1,500–2,500 sq ft, 2,500–4,000 sq ft, or 4,000+ sq ft. A studio that focuses on large villas in Sarjapur can immediately prioritize a 3,500 sq ft request over a 1,000 sq ft remodel.

### 2. Physical location and property name
In cities like Bengaluru or Mumbai, geography dictates logistics, site supervision costs, and delivery margins. Asking for the specific apartment development (for instance, "Prestige Falcon City" or "Adarsh Palm Retreat") tells you the builder's base handover standard and whether your carpentry teams have worked in that society before.

### 3. Handover date and target move-in timeline
Interior timelines in India are notorious for slippage because civil handovers get delayed. Asking when keys will be handed over separates ready buyers from homeowners who will not break ground for eighteen months.
- Keys in hand / Ready to start immediately
- Possession expected in 1–3 months
- Possession expected in 3–6 months
- Just exploring ideas (Possession > 6 months)

If someone selects "Just exploring", send them your automated design preparation guide rather than booking an in-person site visit.

### 4. Realistic investment range
This is where most designers hesitate. They worry that asking for a budget will scare clients away. In reality, affluent homeowners have a budget in mind; they want to know whether you can work within it.

Provide realistic ranges that reflect your actual project minimums:
- ₹15 Lakhs – ₹25 Lakhs (Entry turnkey)
- ₹25 Lakhs – ₹45 Lakhs (Premium custom)
- ₹45 Lakhs – ₹75 Lakhs (Luxury bespoke)
- Above ₹75 Lakhs (Full architectural overhaul)

If your minimum engagement is ₹25 Lakhs, do not offer an option below ₹20 Lakhs. Letting a homeowner select ₹8 Lakhs only creates an awkward rejection conversation forty-eight hours later.

### 5. Architectural drawings and floor plans
Allow a direct file upload for PDF floor plans or builder brochures. A prospect who takes the trouble to find their floor plan and attach it is ninety percent more likely to sign a contract than someone filling out a form on their commute.

## How to present the questionnaire without killing mobile conversions

Do not present all fifteen fields on a single, intimidating screen. A wall of inputs looks like homework on a smartphone.

Use a multi-step progressive form:
- Step 1: Property Type & Location (2 taps)
- Step 2: Carpet Area & Possession Date (2 taps)
- Step 3: Budget Bracket (1 tap)
- Step 4: Floor Plan Upload (Optional)
- Step 5: Contact Details (Name, WhatsApp number, Email)

Putting the contact fields on the final step dramatically lifts completion rates. By the time the user enters their phone number, they have already invested ninety seconds answering project questions; psychologically, they want to see their submission go through.
