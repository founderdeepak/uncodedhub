# Making Websites Win — Principles

## Top 5 — Quick Reference
- Diagnose before you redesign — most website "improvements" are guesses; real conversion gains come from evidence about why real visitors leave
- Use "method marketing": become your own customer (try to buy from yourself and competitors) to feel the actual friction points
- A/B test every significant change — belief in an idea is not evidence it converts better
- Make benefits explicit and concrete — visitors won't infer value, you have to state it directly
- Remove risk with strong guarantees — fear of commitment kills conversions more than price does

## Full Principles (16 points)
- Follow DiPS: Diagnose → Problem → Solution — never jump straight to "solutions" (redesigns, new copy) without first diagnosing what's actually broken with real visitor data #core
- "Meek tweaking" (small cosmetic changes made on a hunch) rarely moves the needle — bigger, evidence-backed changes produce the outsized wins #core
- Use multiple diagnostic tools in combination — analytics, click maps, session recordings, on-page surveys, and user tests each reveal different blind spots; no single tool tells the whole story #core
- "Method marketing": go through your own buying funnel as if you were a real customer, and do the same with competitors — you'll surface friction you're blind to as the business owner #core
- Talk to real customers directly (phone calls, live chat, exit surveys) — their actual words often become your best headline and copy, better than anything you'd write internally #core
- A winning page must be well-written in the visitor's language, not internal jargon — if visitors don't understand it in seconds, they leave #core
- A winning page must be genuinely easy to use — friction in navigation or forms loses far more customers than weak copy does #core
- Make sure the offer actually gives visitors what they want — diagnose whether you're solving the right problem before optimizing how you present the offer #core
- State benefits explicitly and concretely — don't assume visitors will connect a feature to its benefit themselves; spell out what changes in their life #core
- Build genuinely irresistible offers, not just persuasive copy around a mediocre one — offer strength often matters more than the words wrapping it #core
- Build credibility deliberately: testimonials, proof, specificity, and third-party validation reduce visitor wariness before they'll commit #core
- Use strong guarantees to remove the visitor's fear of commitment — risk reversal converts hesitant buyers who are sold but scared #core
- Design multistep funnels ("water chutes") that serve both early-stage (just browsing) and late-stage (ready to buy) visitors simultaneously, rather than one page for everyone #core
- For complex sales, break the decision into manageable steps rather than asking for one big commitment — complexity itself is a conversion killer #minor
- Always measure changes with A/B testing — a change that "feels" better is not the same as a change that's proven to convert better #core
- Lifetime Customer Value (LCV) is a conversion lever people forget — increasing what a customer is worth long-term can matter more than optimizing the first purchase alone #minor
