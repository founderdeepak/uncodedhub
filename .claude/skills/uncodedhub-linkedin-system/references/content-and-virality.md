## 1. Carrier Trust — 7 elements

LinkedIn works for **expensive, considered purchases** (>$500) because buyers
evaluate the human carrying the offer, not just the offer itself. The "biggest lie
in personal branding": fame = trust (reality-TV contestants have huge views, no
trust). Build these into content over time:

1. **You've done it** — lived credibility beats claimed expertise; can't be faked
   or AI-generated. If not yet true for something specific, substitute effort
   ("I did 200 hours of research" / "I audited 20 coach sites this week").
2. **Why you care** — state the real motive plainly, even bluntly. Ambiguity about
   motive breeds distrust; honesty builds it.
3. **Real-life mess** — show imperfection; humans don't bond with robots or
   perfect ad copy. Filter test: *"Would I be comfortable saying this to a table
   of 12 people at dinner?"* If not, don't post it — don't force vulnerability
   past your own comfort.
4. **Same every time** — identical on camera/in writing as in person. Faked
   opinions for engagement are the actual cause of "creator burnout."
5. **Skin in the game** — something to lose or something costly done (long live
   calls, real financial/time commitments). Can't be faked.
6. **Ahead, not above** — status-signal relatability, not superiority (no
   Lambo/private-jet flexing — attracts narcissists or vulnerable people, the
   classic MLM dynamic). Share the blue-collar/non-glamorous origin story.
7. **Pick a side** — a genuine, non-political/non-religious point of view worth
   being disliked for. No discernible position = no trust.

## 2. The Six Gates of Trust

Trust is a spectrum from **Complete Stranger** to **Identity Ally** (refers you
unprompted — the real growth engine). Gates open **sequentially** — skipping ahead
(e.g. a huge guarantee before someone thinks you're credible) fails.

1. **Is this real?** — content/DMs feel human and specific, not generic AI output
   or a too-good offer.
2. **Is this for me?** — interest/tribal alignment; personalization beats
   broadcast.
3. **Special snowflake** — "but do you understand *my* specific situation?"
   Solved by niching, not generic expertise. *(Gates 1–3 = the "Panadol" phase —
   proving you understand their external reality.)*
4. **Risk** — social proof, case studies, understanding their *internal*
   reality; this is where sales calls typically happen.
5. **Now** — inertia/urgency/closing tactics/guarantees. Only works once 1–4 are
   open — a guarantee thrown at a stranger who thinks you're "dodgy" does nothing.
6. **You (identity ally)** — post-delivery satisfaction turning a client (or even
   a non-paying follower who got value) into someone who refers you unprompted.

**Referral insight**: referrals convert easily because the referrer pre-opens
Gates 1–4 for you. Cold content/DM marketing has to do that work itself — that's
*why* it feels harder than referrals, not because it doesn't work.

**Trust Utility Equation**: decision ≈ **Perceived Likelihood × Value − Risk**. A
strong personal brand raises perceived likelihood, raises perceived value
(clearly understands the situation), and lowers perceived risk — all
subconsciously, before any hard-sell tactic.

**Panadol vs. Fish Oil**: lead with content/offers solving an acutely *felt* pain
(Panadol — headache, act now) over abstract long-term good advice (Fish Oil —
nobody urgently buys prevention). Only introduce "fish oil" once trust exists.

## 3. Hook = Movie Trailer (4 parts)

Spend roughly half the writing time on the hook (first 2 lines + image) — its
only job is to earn the "…more" click.

1. **Setting & Cast** — reference a recognizable person/brand/event (borrows
   their trust). Essential with no brand of your own yet — piggyback on notable
   figures, news, or your own accomplishments.
2. **Story & Genre** — sets the reader's expectation of payoff.
3. **Conflict/Tension** — opens a curiosity loop (a dinner-table story
   interrupted — nobody looks away until it resolves).
4. **Director's Cut** — your unique opinion/lived take no AI or Google search can
   produce. The only truly future-proof differentiator as AI content floods feeds.

**First-principles test**: could this hook be Googled or ChatGPT'd? If yes, it's
commodity and underperforms.

**"Paint their house"**: describe their literal environment/objects (tools,
commute, screen, bag) using tribal nouns — creates involuntary, pre-verbal
recognition ("holy that's me") stronger than any generic clickbait hook.

## 4. Hook-mechanics stats (850-post study)

First 1–3 lines, ~129 characters median before "see more" — the hook decides
whether anyone reads the post at all.

- **Numbers win**: 62% of top hooks lead with a number or stat.
- **Quoted language works**: 43% use a quote or quoted phrase.
- **Questions/"how-to" framing**: ~14% of hooks — engage the reader's need to
  close an open loop.
- **Bold/contrarian statements** surprise the reader out of scroll-mode.
- **First-person openers** ("I…"): ~26–28% of hooks — far more common than
  starting with "You" (~3%). Lead with your story, pivot to the reader later.
- **Layer tactics**: 95% of top hooks use 2+ techniques together; 67% use three
  (number + contrarian claim + question, etc.). A single flat tactic
  underperforms a stacked one.
- **Tone**: short, punchy, conversational fragments — not corporate prose. Mild
  bluntness signals a real human voice, not a press release.
- **Exclamation points/hype are rare** (~4%) — factual, curiosity-driven hooks
  beat hype-driven ones.

**The single biggest factor separating viral trust-building posts from flops**:
the hook makes a specific, credible promise, and the body actually pays it off.
Bait-and-switch posts lose trust; unhooked value posts never get read. 67% of top
posts stacked 3+ hook tactics, and every top post fulfilled its hook's promise.

## 5. Structural elements of the body

- **Short paragraphs, 1–2 sentences, heavy white space.** Often one sentence per
  line. No walls of text.
- **Numbered lists/bullets/all-caps section headers** (BACKGROUND, CHALLENGE,
  RESULT) to signal organization and skimmability.
- **Story-lesson structure**: a short narrative (failure, experiment, client
  story) followed by an explicit takeaway — not anecdote for its own sake.
- **Concrete, specific advice**, not platitudes — numbered, actionable steps.
- **Payoff discipline**: if the hook promises 4 things, deliver exactly 4. Top
  posts never bait-and-switch — this is where trust is won or lost.
- **Minimal but intentional visual cues** — an emoji as a directional pointer, a
  checkmark, occasional caps for headers. Decoration is never the point;
  clarity is.

## 6. Psychological & emotional triggers

This layer turns "engagement" into "I'd hire this person":

1. **Authority bias/social proof** — real numbers, results, credentials answer
   "why should I listen to you?" before it's asked.
2. **Reciprocity** — real value or a free resource, no strings (or a low-friction
   comment ask); readers feel obligated to reciprocate later, often by buying.
3. **Pain + aspiration (PAS arc)** — name the reader's fear/frustration/stuckness
   explicitly, then show the path to a better state. Seen, then motivated.
4. **Novelty/FOMO** — cutting-edge experiments, contrarian takes, "nobody's done
   this before" claims trigger fear of being left behind.
5. **Authenticity and vulnerability** — admitting mistakes/failure/being
   underestimated builds more trust than a polished highlight reel. A little
   polarization repels non-buyers and magnetizes the right ones.
6. **Narrative transportation/identification** — first-person or "you" scenarios
   let the reader mentally insert themselves ("if he did it, maybe I can too") —
   what makes advice *persuasive*, not just informative.

## 7. 8 viral post patterns — ranked for Uncoded Hub

Two data points to keep in mind for every pattern: **9 of 10 top posts use
"I/me/my/you"** (first-person, direct address beats third-person "brochure"
writing), and **the first 2–3 lines decide everything.**

### 1. Lead Magnet / Offer Post — best single pick
~biggest driver of outlier (top 1%) engagement — trades a comment for value,
compounding visibility. Structure: pain-point hook → introduce the free resource
in one line → 3 bullets of what's inside → simple comment-to-receive CTA →
optional urgency → tag-a-friend close.
*Adapting*: "Free: a 15-point 'Is Your Coaching Website Costing You Clients?'
audit checklist — comment 'AUDIT'," or "I built a free AI-chatbot-readiness
scorecard for coaches, comment 'SCORE.'" Run every 2–3 weeks, rotating the offer.

### 2. Contrarian Opinion / Myth-Busting
Bold contrarian hook (flat negation or "NOT") → name the common belief, admit you
used to hold it → reasoning/mini-story + a stat → real perspective → invite
debate. Even disagreement counts as engagement that boosts distribution.
*Adapting*: "Your website is NOT your marketing strategy," "Most coaches don't
have a visibility problem — they have a conversion problem."

### 3. Personal Story With a Lesson (~27% of all viral posts — single most common
format)
Hook (confession/near-failure) → context/stakes → struggle → turning point →
resolution with numbers → one-line lesson → CTA question. Vulnerability builds
trust faster than any expertise claim.
*Adapting*: the agency founder-story ("almost lost our biggest client because our
website looked like a hobby project"), or retell a client's transformation in
first person.

### 4. How-To / Educational Listicle (~17–18% of viral posts)
Hook naming the outcome + missing obstacle → one-line credibility → numbered
steps (each with a one-sentence result) → encouragement → CTA. Establishes
authority, immediately actionable, save-worthy.
*Adapting*: "5 things every coach's website needs before you spend $1 on ads,"
"3 SEO mistakes killing your coaching business's visibility." Root each step in a
real client-work number.

### 5. Numbered "Lessons/Myths I Learned" Listicle
"[N] things I learned/unlearned about [topic]" → old assumption vs. new reality →
numbered insights (1–2 sentences each) → wrap-up → invite readers to add their
own. Lower lift, still reinforces expertise, invites comments.
*Adapting*: "6 things I learned after building 100+ websites for consultants."

### 6. Short, Punchy Cliffhanger Opener (a hook technique, not a standalone
format)
A one-to-four-word first line alone on its own paragraph, then a short context
line. Maximizes intrigue-per-character before "See more." Layer onto #1 and #3.
*Adapting*: "We lost the client. \n\n On day one."

### 7. Problem/Solution ("Struggling to X?") Hook
Name the reader's exact pain in a question → promise relief immediately → deliver
the how. Works as both hook and rehook. Best used as an opener for #4 or #2, not
alone.

### 8. Timely Trend / Industry Commentary — lowest priority
React to a current event/platform change → what it means for the audience →
quick take/action item. Requires speed and genuine timeliness; lowest priority of
the eight unless posting reactively within 24–48 hours.

**Priority order for Uncoded Hub**: 1 (Lead Magnet) → 2 (Contrarian) → 3
(Personal Story) → 4 (How-To) → 5 (Lessons Listicle) → 6 (Cliffhanger, as a
layered technique) → deprioritized: 7 and 8 as standalone pillars.

## 8. Idea generation — the swipe file / Netflix Principle

**"Steal the format, color it in"**: don't invent from a blank page. Build a swipe
file using LinkedIn's Save (⋯ menu → Save) on outlier posts (unusually high
engagement relative to that person's baseline). When writing, reuse the
*structure* of a proven hook/post with specifics — not the words, the skeleton. A
working format can be reused 5–15+ times over months with different specifics
(e.g. "I spent [X time] breaking down [notable brand]'s funnel," reused across
clients, both going viral repeatedly).

## 9. CTA techniques

Overt selling ("buy now") tanks engagement — zero top-decile posts pitch
directly.

- **"Comment X for the free resource"** — drives algorithmic reach (comments >
  likes for distribution) AND captures a warm, self-identified lead to DM. The
  single most leverage-dense CTA pattern found.
- **Discussion invitations** ("What's your take?") — lower-friction, builds
  relationship without asking for anything transactional.
- **Rare, soft follow/connect asks** — top creators almost never beg for follows.
- **Casual, story-embedded product mentions** ("a client closed $40K using my
  framework") — social proof that plugs the offer without pitching directly.
- **Transparent generosity** ("no comment required, just grab it") — builds extra
  trust when used occasionally.

**Engagement compounding**: on LinkedIn (unlike Instagram/TikTok), a comment can
surface in *their* network's feed alongside the post — debates/disagreements in
comments actively distribute content further. Deliberately writing a mildly
contrarian, debatable claim in the niche's territory (not politics/religion) is a
legitimate engagement tactic.

## 10. Posting cadence — the daily 1-10-10 system

The single highest-leverage system in the whole training:

- **1 post** every weekday (Mon–Fri) — forces craft improvement, gives the
  algorithm consistent tribal signal.
- **10 comments/day** on others' posts — cheap reps at having an opinion
  publicly; a legitimate stand-alone lead source on its own.
- **10 DMs/day** (new outreach, connection requests, or follow-ups all count) —
  described as the single highest-leverage habit; one tested case built a $100K
  business from zero content, using DMs alone.

Volume math: 10 DMs/day × ~250 working days = ~2,500 conversations initiated per
year.

## 11. Monthly offer campaigns — Florence / Wine Tour / Butter Boy

Daily posting/commenting/DMing *deposits* trust; once a month, run a low-pressure
invitational campaign that *bridges* accumulated trust into a sales conversation
without ever feeling like a hard pitch (recipients can say yes/no without losing
any trust equity). Three formats, increasing complexity:

1. **"Florence" (1:1)** — a single personalized invite ("I've got 3 slots this
   month — want me to walk you through how we got [similar client] X result?").
   Zero setup — no landing page, just a DM.
2. **"Wine tour" (small group, ~12 people)** — roundtables, lunches, small live
   breakdowns for a curated cohort.
3. **"Butter Boy" (30–100+ people)** — a workshop, webinar, or paid challenge
   (e.g. a $99 5-day challenge that up-sells into a 3-month retainer).

**Campaign design rule**: pick 3 variables — WHO (the tribe), WHAT format
(Florence/Wine Tour/Butter Boy), the PROBLEM addressed — then change only *one*
variable per month.

**Suggested first 3 months**: Month 1 Florence (1:1 audits), Month 2 Wine Tour
(small-group teardown of 10–12 coach sites), Month 3 Butter Boy (paid
mini-challenge or webinar upselling into the Coach Authority System).

## 12. Full pre-publish checklist

Run every Uncoded Hub post through this before it goes live. Any "no" is a
required rewrite, not a note to self.

**Hook (first 3 lines / ~130 characters)**
- Specific number/stat, OR bold/contrarian claim, OR genuine personal
  story-open ("I...")?
- At least 2 hook tactics stacked?
- Under 3 lines before "see more," zero throat-clearing?
- Would the tribe feel a specific itch to know more (not vague intrigue)?
- Is the voice actually yours — blunt, alive — not sanitized corporate-speak?

**Promise vs. Payoff**
- If the hook promises N things, does the body deliver exactly N, clearly
  numbered?
- If the hook opens a story loop, does it resolve — no bait-and-switch?
- One genuinely non-obvious, applicable takeaway — not a recycled platitude?

**Structure & Skimmability**
- 1–2 sentence paragraphs, generous white space?
- Bulleted/numbered breakdown wherever there's a list?
- Fully graspable by skimming in 10 seconds?
- Jargon/filler/hedge-words cut on the edit pass?

**Psychological Layer — pick at least 2 deliberately**
- Authority/proof — a real result/number/credential?
- Reciprocity — genuinely useful, free, no strings (or one low-friction comment)?
- Pain-to-aspiration arc — a real fear/frustration, then a better state?
- Authenticity — an honest admission/failure/vulnerable beat?
- Identification — written so the reader can picture themselves in it?

**Call-to-Action**
- Ends with *some* interaction prompt — comment-for-resource, a real question,
  or an invite to reply — never a hard pitch?
- If offering a freebie, is the ask (a specific comment word) clear and
  low-friction?
- Product/service mention embedded naturally inside a proof point, never stated
  as a pitch?
- Would this CTA actually generate a DM-able, self-identified warm lead?

**Brand & Consistency**
- Inside Uncoded Hub's core lane?
- Voice matches the last 5–10 posts?
- Reveals expertise by *showing* a method/result, not claiming one?
- Comfortable mildly polarizing the wrong-fit reader for the right one?

**Final Gut Check**
- Would a coach with a $10K+ budget, reading only this post, think "this person
  clearly knows what they're doing"?
- Anything reading as pure hype with no substance? Cut it.
- Every line serving the hook's promise, the story, or the CTA?

**The one-line takeaway**: hook with something specific and credible → keep every
promise in the body → make the reader feel understood, not sold to → end with a
low-friction way to raise their hand.

## 13. Task recipe: building a content plan

1. Confirm the sub-niche and its tribal vocabulary are settled
   (`niche-and-tribe.md`) — don't write tribal content against an undefined tribe.
2. Rotate across pillars in this priority order (§7): lead magnet offer,
   contrarian/myth-busting, personal story, how-to, lessons listicle — with
   cliffhanger openers layered onto story/myth posts.
3. For each post, pick a hook structure from §3–4 and name which one is used.
4. Map each post to a Gate from §2 it's meant to advance.
5. Run every draft through the full pre-publish checklist in §12 before calling
   it done.
6. Slot the daily 1-10-10 cadence (§10) around the posts, and check where in the
   monthly Florence/Wine Tour/Butter Boy cycle (§11) this week's content sits.
