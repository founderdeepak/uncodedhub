## 1. The 5 Rules of Value (the selection filter)

A lead magnet's worth is entirely subjective — worth exactly what the prospect
*thinks* it's worth. Run every idea through:

1. **Scarcity** — insider knowledge, not Googleable? ("The exact system we use to
   close $5K website deals — not shared publicly.")
2. **Relevance** — solves *one specific* problem for a specific ICP, not a broad
   topic?
3. **Immediate Utility** — usable in minutes, no learning curve?
4. **Time Savings** — shortcuts effort/decision-making/research?
5. **Money** — tied to a quantifiable financial outcome (revenue gained, cost
   avoided, deals saved)?

**Checklist**: the best lead magnets hit all five. Hitting only one or two = go
back and sharpen before building.

## 2. What kills lead magnets

- **Starting with the format.** "I'll make an ebook/checklist/quiz" is the
  single most emphasized mistake — decide the *value* first, format last.
- **Irrelevant lead magnets.** Chasing likes/comments/followers with something
  disconnected from the actual offer attracts the wrong crowd, never converts.
- **Being lazy.** A thin resource with a flashy hook gets downloaded once and
  destroys trust the moment it's opened — worse than offering nothing.
- **Analysis paralysis or the "Hail Mary."** Either overthink and never ship, or
  spend weeks on a "perfect" untested thing that flops and kills confidence in
  lead magnets entirely.

## 3. Turning existing knowledge into a magnet

1. **Nail the ICP first.** Everything downstream depends on knowing exactly who
   this is for.
2. **Look at what's already being done.** What's already being solved (even for
   your own clients)? What do people keep asking? What's already tested to work?
3. **Slice off one piece — don't teach everything.** The one thing already
   delivered, that consistently gets results, that people would pay just to
   know.
4. **Two paths to build it**: *Innovate/Repurpose* (package existing
   knowledge/tools into something new) or *Curate* ("steal like an artist" —
   aggregate best practices, add your spin, credit if it boosts credibility).
   Combining both is where the best magnets come from.

**Idea-framing template** (test if an idea is sharp enough):
> "The [Lead Magnet Name] is for [ICP] struggling to [specific problem]. It shows
> you how to [secret sauce] in [time saved] with [immediate utility] — [money
> saved/made] while solving [relevance to pain point]."

If this can't be filled in cleanly, the idea isn't specific enough yet.

**Final 5-point filter before building**: (1) fits the template above cleanly?
(2) solves a problem the ICP actually cares about? (3) hits the 5 Rules of
Value? (4) aligns with what's actually sold (the magnet solves *part* of the
problem, the paid offer solves the rest)? (5) can it be built fast without
losing value?

## 4. Validate before you build

Unless it's repurposable in under 1–2 hours, **test demand before creating
anything** — 99% of people skip this, which is why most lead magnets flop.

1. **Research & conversations** — mine what the ICP already says in real
   conversations, LinkedIn comments, forums, search queries.
2. **Test in DMs with pre-loaded value** — float it directly: *"Sounds like
   you're wrestling with [problem] — we've nailed this so well we're thinking of
   packaging it into a [format] to give away. Want first dibs?"*
3. **Test in the LinkedIn Featured section** — place a preview, see if it gets
   clicks before building the full thing.
4. **Post a teaser/waitlist** — gauge interest with "early access" before the
   resource exists.
5. **Test via a squeeze page** — if the website already gets traffic, a simple
   landing page tells you fast whether people want it.

## 5. Choosing a format + structuring the content

**Format genuinely doesn't matter**, as long as it (1) clearly communicates the
value and is easy to consume, and (2) is easy/fast/comfortable *to create*. Play
to strengths — write if writing is easy, record video if speaking is strong.
Categories: written (guides, checklists, case studies, scripts/templates, audit
tools), visual (infographics, process maps, Figma templates), video/audio
(walkthroughs, teardowns, mini-courses), interactive (quizzes, calculators,
scorecards, worksheets), live (workshops, AMAs, strategy calls), tool/resource
(spreadsheets, dashboards, prompt libraries, swipe files). **Audit tools,
scorecards, and calculators are explicitly named** — directly usable for a
website/chatbot/SEO agency.

**Every lead magnet, regardless of format, follows one outline:**
1. **Explanatory notes/intro** — what it is, why useful, a hook/story, the
   outcome, how to use it fast.
2. **Main content** — the actual insight/framework step-by-step, tied back to
   the paid offer, a soft nudge toward next steps (no hard sell yet).
3. **Selling next steps** — short bio (why listen to you), social proof, a clear
   CTA, optional links.

**Templates for the final section**:
- Credibility: *"Since launching [X], we've helped [N] [ICP] achieve [result]."*
- Qualification: *"This works best for [ICP] who are [stage/problem]. If that's
  you, here's the next step..."*
- CTA options: book a call / follow for more / download a deeper resource.
- Optional urgency: *"DM me '[keyword]' in the next [timeframe] for [bonus]."*

## 6. The Comment → DM → Delivery flow

The mechanism behind Deepak's already-best-performing post pattern ("comment X
for the resource"):

1. **Hook** — a pattern-interrupt opener: *"I spent [X hours] breaking down how
   [person/company] achieved [outcome]"* or *"I've helped [audience] achieve
   [outcome] using [tool] — now I'm giving it away for free."*
2. **What's inside** — 3–4 bullets naming specific insights/tools/outcomes, not
   vague promises.
3. **The ask** — exactly two frictionless steps: **(1) Comment "[keyword]"**
   and **(2) Connect with me** (DMs only reach connections on LinkedIn — this
   line matters mechanically, not just rhetorically).
4. **Delivery promise** — "I'll send it directly to your DMs."
5. **Bonus/repost lever (optional)** — "Repost this and I'll include [extra
   bonus]" — extends reach for free.

Three named templates: *Authority & Transformation*, *Lead Magnet Teaser*,
*Results-Oriented Case Study* — same skeleton, different opening frame.

**Visuals are non-negotiable** — every post needs a supporting image/GIF
previewing the resource, mobile-optimized (~800×1000px, fills the phone
viewport).

**Once in the DM — the "sell-by-chat" logic**: deliver the lead magnet as
promised, then use "preloaded value" (extra relevant insight dropped in-chat) to
move skepticism → curiosity → trust → booked call. The lead magnet is the
opening move of a conversation, not the end of one. (Full DM mechanics in
`dms-and-selling.md`.)

**Evergreen layer**: don't rely only on one-off posts — a preview in the
LinkedIn **Featured section** with a clear "Get the free guide" CTA, backed by a
dedicated **squeeze page**, captures traffic between posts (see
`profile-optimization.md` §5).

## 7. 5 ready-made lead magnet ideas for the Coach Authority System

Applying the 5 Rules + format logic to coaches/consultants who need a website, AI
chatbot, and SEO:

1. **"The 12-Point Coach Website Audit Checklist"** (written/audit-tool).
   *Relevance*: names exactly what coaches get wrong (no booking CTA, no
   chatbot, invisible on Google). *Utility*: self-score today. *Money*: "each
   unchecked box is likely costing you booked calls."
2. **"The AI Chatbot Readiness Scorecard"** (interactive quiz/calculator).
   8–10 questions about the current site/funnel → a score + 2–3 specific gaps.
   Strongest fit for the "interactive" category; doubles as a soft-sell into the
   chatbot component — the scorecard itself demonstrates the AI capability being
   sold.
3. **"5-Minute Website + SEO Teardown"** (personalized Loom video). A short
   teardown of a prospect's actual site, offered via DM after they comment —
   maps directly to the "test in DMs with pre-loaded value" tactic and the
   sell-by-chat approach: show, don't tell.
4. **"The Coach's DM Script Swipe File"** (template/script bank). Copy-paste
   scripts coaches can use to convert their own traffic into booked calls — high
   perceived value because it's outside Uncoded Hub's core service but adjacent
   to the outcome coaches want.
5. **"The Coach Authority System Blueprint"** (one-page visual/process map). A
   before/after diagram showing how website + chatbot + SEO combine into one
   client-acquisition system — the "evergreen" magnet for the Featured section,
   entry point of the value ladder.

Each idea should run through validation (§4) before full production.

## 8. Email: the core philosophy + full funnel architecture

**Central bet**: content and DMs generate conversations, but email is what turns
"not ready yet" into "ready in 3 months." Most leads aren't ready to buy the
moment they opt in — without a structured system they evaporate. Email is the
holding pattern that keeps a lead warm without manual chasing.

**Three principles**:
1. **Leads, not likes.** Content's job is starting conversations/capturing
   emails-DMs, not engagement. Email is downstream — where captured attention
   converts on a schedule.
2. **Invite, don't pitch.** Every offer is "would you like to join us?" not a
   hard sell — soft, low-friction CTAs (reply with a keyword) over checkout links.
3. **Implementation beats perfection.** Launch the sequence as written; don't
   endlessly tweak subject lines first. Consistency/cadence beat any single
   "perfect" email.

**Funnel architecture:**
```
Lead Magnet Offer → Opt-In (LinkedIn post/profile/DM) → Lead Magnet Delivered
Immediately → 14-Day Email Nurture (6 emails) → Weekly Cadence (3/week, ongoing)
→ Monthly Offer Campaign (Whisper → Tease → Shout) → Structured Follow-Up (AIDA)
```
Deliberately simple enough to run with one person and a plain-text email tool —
no complex marketing automation needed.

## 9. The 14-Day Nurture Sequence

Six emails, fixed jobs, fixed order:

| Email | Day | Job | Mechanic |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 1 | Deliver the lead magnet + set expectations | Thank them, deliver value immediately, preview "3 challenges" coming (pre-frames the next 3 emails, curiosity about which applies to *them*). Soft CTA: reply one fixed tracking keyword. |
| 2 | Day 2 | Get a reply — nothing else | One trivially easy question ("what type of clients do you work with?"). Turns a passive subscriber into someone who's taken an action. Disguised as a personal, non-marketing email. |
| 3, 4, 5 | Days 5, 9, 12 | Address Problem 1, 2, 3 | Each uses **Problem → Agitate → Solution (PAS)**: name it, show why it hurts, give a mini-solution (a Loom, a tip, a client story). Must be a "painkiller," not a "vitamin." Ends with a P.S. soft-offer tied to that pain. |
| 6 | Day 14 | Invite them to work with you | Summarizes what they've learned, asks if they're serious about solving X, frames the offer as scarce/curated ("small private group," "2 more private clients"). CTA: reply with keyword. |

**Explicit goal of the 14 days**: capture the ~3% ready to buy right now.
Everyone else "graduates" into the weekly cadence, not dropped.

---

## 10. The ongoing weekly cadence (after Day 14)

Three emails per week, indefinitely, fixed rotation:
- **Story + Lesson** (e.g. Monday) — a personal/vulnerable anecdote (a pet
  running away, a wedding speech, a vet bill) pivoting into a lesson tied to the
  product. Soft CTA.
- **Case Study / Social Proof** (e.g. Wednesday) — a client transformation via
  PAS (their before, why it hurt, how working together fixed it). Soft CTA.
- **Free Resource / Extra Value** (e.g. Friday) — a new template/Loom/checklist
  generating a reply, opening a 1:1 conversation.

**3/week is a tested ceiling, not a floor** — cut to 1/week if unsustainable.
Consistency of cadence matters more than volume.

## 11. Monthly offer campaign — Whisper → Tease → Shout

Because the list keeps growing and readiness changes, one deliberate sales push a
month, escalating over 4 weeks:
- **Weeks 1–3 (Whisper)**: soft CTAs embedded in regular story/case-study/
  resource emails — no separate "sales email" yet.
- **Week 4, first email (Tease)**: short, plain-text "something's coming" email
  purely for anticipation.
- **Week 4, last email (Shout)**: direct, friendly invite ("I'm working with X
  people next month who want Y — want to join?").

Each month **rotates** which avatar, which problem, and which offer format
(direct offer / live challenge / free audit) gets featured — same list, different
pitch each month, reducing fatigue and increasing relevance to different
subsegments.

---

## 12. The AIDA follow-up system

When someone replies to an offer but doesn't book, apply AIDA (Attention →
Interest → Desire → Action): send a short doc or 2-option time slot to move
interest into a booked call, then run up to **4 follow-ups over 1–2 weeks** if
they go quiet — each deliberately casual/personal (weaving in real-life detail
like "taking Friday off for my daughter's recital") rather than sounding
automated. The last one explicitly gives a graceful, low-pressure exit ("no
worries either way, I'll check back in a few months").

## 13. Copywriting techniques used throughout

From the ~50 real, dated email templates in the source's swipe file:

- **Curiosity-driven, benefit-vague subject lines**: numbers/specifics
  ("8,400,000 views," "$70,000 in 5 weeks," "179 meetings in 30 days") rather
  than generic subjects — the number is the hook.
- **Personal story as the Trojan horse.** Most emails open with an unrelated
  personal anecdote (a run, a dog, a wedding, a vet bill) and pivot mid-email
  into the business lesson/offer — reads as a real person's life update, not
  marketing copy, and is genuinely memorable in a crowded inbox.
- **One-word reply CTAs.** Nearly every email ends with "reply with [KEYWORD]"
  rather than a link — trackable per campaign, lowers commitment friction vs. a
  landing page, starts a real 1:1 conversation the sender can sell in.
- **P.S. as the second CTA.** Almost every template carries a P.S. reframing or
  adding a second, lower-commitment offer.
- **PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution)** for nurture-problem and case-study emails.
- **AIDA** specifically for the reply-but-not-booked follow-up thread.
- **Scarcity/exclusivity framing** ("small private group," "2 more private
  clients," qualifying criteria) rather than open enrollment — signals
  selectivity, raises perceived value.
- **Anti-guru positioning.** Several emails explicitly attack "brokefluencer"
  advice (comment for 2 hours a day, post for 6 months) to differentiate as more
  substantive — a contrarian trust-builder.
- **The LVQ framework** (also used in DMs, see `dms-and-selling.md` §4) —
  directly portable to email replies once someone responds.
- **Rotating avatar/problem/offer** monthly to keep the list fresh and speak to
  different segments over time.

## 14. The mapped funnel for Uncoded Hub

Deepak's current system is LinkedIn content + DMs → trust → pitch, with **no
email layer**. This blueprint bolts on exactly where a LinkedIn lead magnet is
delivered by email instead of (or alongside) DM delivery:

```
LinkedIn post/profile/DM → lead magnet opt-in (email captured)
   ↓
Email 1: deliver the lead magnet + preview 3 problems coaches face getting
          clients (no website that converts, no way to qualify leads 24/7,
          invisible in AI search)
   ↓
14-day nurture (Emails 2–6) using PAS on the 3 core pains The Coach Authority
   System solves: (1) a site that doesn't convert traffic into booked calls,
   (2) no way to capture/qualify leads when the coach is offline, (3)
   invisibility in AI-driven search (SEO/LLM visibility) — each with a mini
   case study or Loom
   ↓
Weekly cadence (3x/week): Story+Lesson / Case Study / Free Resource — recycling
   existing LinkedIn content and client wins into email instead of writing
   net-new material
   ↓
Monthly Whisper→Tease→Shout campaign pitching Coach Authority System cohorts,
   rotating: avatar (life coach vs. business consultant vs. therapist), problem
   emphasized (conversion / lead capture / AI visibility), offer format (direct
   offer / free audit call / live demo)
   ↓
AIDA follow-up for replies that don't book — reusing the DM follow-up cadence
   and tone from `dms-and-selling.md` so email and DM speak with one voice
```

**Why this fits**: extends the shelf life of a lead past the DM conversation's
natural end — a coach who says "not now" in DM drops into the 14-day sequence,
then weekly/monthly cadence, automatically. The "reply with a keyword" mechanic
maps directly onto the DM-based selling motion. The 3-pain, rotate-monthly
structure fits because The Coach Authority System genuinely bundles three
distinct value props (conversion, lead capture, SEO/AI visibility) — each can
anchor its own month. Since Deepak sells globally, email's async, time-zone-
agnostic nature keeps leads in different regions nurtured on autopilot.

**What to adapt, not copy verbatim**: the source author's voice is very
casual/Australian/bro-ish — keep the *mechanics* (story-first, PAS, one-keyword
CTA, P.S. offer) but rewrite in a tone fitting a B2B agency serving
coaches/consultants — still personal and story-driven, more polished. Case
studies must be Uncoded Hub's own real client results, not fabricated.

**Fastest path given zero current email infrastructure**: pick one lead magnet
already used in the LinkedIn/DM funnel, set up delivery + the 6-email nurture
sequence first (highest leverage, lowest effort), add the weekly cadence and
monthly campaign only once that's proven.

**The single most important structural principle**: the sequence's power comes
from separating "nurture" from "pitch" into distinct, scheduled phases — the
pitch never appears until real, specific value has been given tied to a real
pain. The 14-day nurture only earns the Day-14 offer after three full emails
have each demonstrated deep understanding of one distinct, painful problem
(PAS, painkiller not vitamin). Every offer after that stays wrapped the same
way — soft weekly, direct once a month, always framed as a scarce invitation.

## 15. Task recipes

**Building a lead magnet:**
1. Confirm the ICP/sub-niche is defined (`niche-and-tribe.md`) before picking a
   topic.
2. Fill the idea-framing template (§3) — if it can't be filled cleanly, sharpen
   the idea first.
3. Run it against the 5 Rules of Value (§1) and the 5-point final filter (§3).
4. Validate before building anything beyond a 1–2 hour repurpose (§4) — pick at
   least one validation method.
5. Pick a format last, not first (§5), matching what's easy to ship.
6. Structure the content in the 3-part outline (§5).
7. Draft the launch post using the Comment→DM→Delivery skeleton (§6).

**Designing an email sequence:**
1. Confirm a lead magnet already exists or is validated (§1–7) — email nurtures
   an opt-in, it doesn't replace one.
2. Map the 3 core pains for the 14-day sequence (§9), each PAS, each a
   painkiller not a vitamin.
3. Set the weekly cadence rotation once Day 14 is live (§10).
4. Plan the next monthly Whisper→Tease→Shout cycle, rotating avatar/problem/
   format from the prior month (§11).
5. Have the AIDA follow-up ready for replies that stall post-offer (§12).
6. Check tone against §14's adaptation note — mechanics from the source, voice
   from Deepak's own.
