## 1. Three DM types + the 7-part/3-layer cold DM template

Three DM types, mapped directly to the Six Gates (`content-and-virality.md` §2) —
treat them differently:

**A. Cold DMs** (target: pass Gate 1, "is this real?")

The **7-part / 3-layer template**:
- *Layer 1 – open the gate*: (1) Tribal recognition (signal shared world/group),
  (2) Honest naming (explicitly call out that it's a cold/random message — grants
  permission, paradoxically increases reply rates), (3) Specificity in their
  words (reference something exact and recent about them).
- *Layer 2 – earn the reply*: (4) Inside-world proof (effortful, costly proof of
  genuinely being in their world — attended their event, watched their recent
  content, did unpaid research), (5) "Unclog your toilet" — deliver unrequested
  value before asking for anything.
- *Layer 3 – remove friction*: (6) The gift (unexpected + useful + optional), (7)
  Permission close ("no biggie if not," "no pressure to reply").

**Golden test**: a good cold DM "would make sense to no one else but this exact
recipient." Generic-but-personalized ("saw you engaged on X, got 15 min for
coffee?") still fails this test.

**B. Intent-based DMs** (target: pass Gate 2, "is this for me?")

Signals: post likes/comments, profile views, connection requests, follows, or
engagement on a *competitor's* similar content (scraping a competitor's viral
lead-magnet comment section for warm prospects is an explicitly used tactic).
Approach: reference the specific thing they engaged with, continue the
conversation from there.

**C. Inbound DMs** (target: move fast through Gate 3, sometimes straight to
close)

**Speed-to-lead is everything** — emotional urgency decays fast (the "balloon"
metaphor: catch the prospect at peak pain before they release it elsewhere —
through drinking, a competitor, or just moving on). Let them vent/share fully
before pitching; only qualify and offer once de-escalated, not mid-vent.

**Practical DM channel tips**: don't send a voice note as the very first message
(feels forced), but use one to warm/re-engage an existing thread (~45 sec,
casual). Segue cold LinkedIn threads to WhatsApp or a phone call when appropriate.
Match channel to the prospect's generation/habits — for Uncoded Hub's often 40+
coach audience, don't be afraid to escalate warm DM threads to a phone/Zoom call.

## 2. Trust Equity = Time × Touches × Type

**Type** ranks from slowest-but-scalable to fastest-but-effortful trust-building:
**Content < Comments < DMs < Voice notes < Phone call < Video call <
In-person.** Content builds awareness at scale; DMs build trust fastest
per-interaction. A creator sitting on a large following but stuck at $10K/month
can often 5–10x revenue purely by DMing everyone who engages with their content —
cited as the highest-leverage unlock in the whole system.

**Realistic expectations**: across thousands of DM threads, the average path to a
booked call took **~26 message exchanges over 1–4 months**. Trust takes time —
don't expect a single templated DM to close deals.

**First move before any new outreach**: mine the existing 14,500 connections'
past likes/comments/profile-views — these are untouched intent-based leads,
lower-hanging fruit than cold prospecting.

## 3. Sell-By-Chat: the Chat-to-Contract Ratio

The core philosophy: **serve, don't sell.** Every message should feel like
solving a problem the prospect already told you about, not pushing an offer. The
sale is "the side effect of being genuinely helpful."

Three excuses the book tells you to kill: *"it feels salesy"* (only true if
spamming canned pitches), *"leads go cold"* (they're not cold, they're
unfollowed-up — most deals close on touch 5+), *"outsourcing doesn't work"* (it
does, but only with a rock-solid written process).

**Chat-to-Contract Ratio** (the book's core KPI):

| Stage | Rate | Cumulative (from 100 chats) |
|---|---|---|
| Reply | 30% | 30 |
| Meaningful engagement | 50% | 15 |
| Call booked | 20% | 3 |
| Show-up | 80% | 2.4 |
| Close | 50% | 1.2 |

~1% of cold contacts become closed deals. Daily baseline: 25–30 contacts/day →
~1 call booked/day → 2–3 sales/week. **Use this to diagnose *where* Deepak's
funnel leaks** (reply problem vs. qualifying problem vs. show-up problem vs.
close problem) rather than treating "low sales" as one undifferentiated problem.

## 4. The conversation flow, stage by stage (Sell-By-Chat)

### Stage 1 — Opener (post-connection or warm-signal trigger)
Never about you — a **noticing**, not an icebreaker. **Precision flattery**:
praise effort/thinking, not outcomes ("Your approach to [X] is EPIC because…") —
proves you actually read their content. **Pattern interrupt**: specific +
emotionally resonant + relevant, never generic ("let's connect," "picking your
brain"). For warm signals, always give **two named paths** to reply:
- Profile view: *"Hey [Name], noticed you checked out my profile. Just curious —
  are you looking for [outcome], or just enjoying the content?"*
- Like: *"Thanks for liking my post on [topic] — are you working on [related
  challenge], or did it just resonate?"*

### Stage 2 — Qualifying
**LVQ Framework**: **L**ube (acknowledge what they said first — "Got it," "That
makes sense" — zero friction) → **V**alue (a genuinely useful insight/tip
*before* asking anything) → **Q**uestion (one open-ended question, one level
deeper).

**A–B Method**: **Point A** (where they are now / the specific pain) vs. **Point
B** (where they want to be). The bigger the gap feels to *them*, the more urgency
they self-generate — use questions to make them articulate the gap, don't state
it yourself.

**Question sequencing rule**: start broad → go deeper → test readiness. Never
stack questions. One question per message. Binary/either-or questions ("was it
[A] or [B]?") outperform open "what's wrong?" questions for reply rate — lower
cognitive cost to reply.

### Stage 3 — Preloaded Value
Before asking for a call/sale, deliver something specific, ungated, immediately
actionable: (1) specific to their world/industry/recent post, (2) ungated — no
opt-in wall, (3) actionable — a checklist/reframe/mini-framework usable today, (4)
tied to the next step — solves one piece so they come back for the rest.
Activates reciprocity — they chase the solution instead of you chasing the sale.
Send a teaser/case-study *before* a booked call so they show up ready to listen.

### Stage 4 — Guiding with Micro-Commitments
1. Small, specific ask: *"Let's lock in a quick 10-minute call — does [day/time]
   work?"* (never open-ended "want to hop on a call sometime?").
2. **Frame work as already underway**: *"I've already started mapping out a few
   ideas we've used to solve [X] for others."* The book's most distinctive
   lever — manufactures investment/reciprocity before any commitment.
3. Link the ask to a tangible outcome, never "let's chat."
4. Break big decisions into small steps: "Why don't I send the details first,
   then we talk?"
5. Always tie back to their stated value/result, never your process.

### Stage 5 — Objection Handling: "No BUTs"
1. **Acknowledge** — *"I hear you, timing is always tricky with [X]."*
2. **Ask for context** — *"What's your biggest concern about [issue] right
   now?"* Usually surfaces the *real* objection (rarely the stated one).
3. **Reassure with proof** — *"One of my clients felt exactly the same about
   [X]. After [action], they got [result]. I think it'd work for you too."*

**The "No BUTs" rule**: never use "but" after acknowledging a concern — it
silently cancels the empathy. Replace with "and," or drop the connector.
- Bad: "I understand your concern, but this could solve it."
- Good: "I understand your concern, and I think this approach could make it
  easier."

### Stage 6 — Closing the Loop
Every conversation — even non-converting ones — ends with a deliberate next
step, never ambiguous.
1. Explicit next step tied to their stated challenge.
2. Propose, don't ask: *"I've penciled in [specific day/time] — let me know if
   that doesn't work."* **Skip the calendar link** — explicitly called a source
   of friction/lost momentum; handle scheduling in-chat.
3. Play the long game when timing is genuinely wrong — name a specific
   re-check-in window, don't disqualify "not now" as "no."
4. Value recap for warm-but-uncommitted leads — restate challenge + proposed
   solution + one clear next step in a single message.

### Stage 7 — Booking → Show-Up
Offer times **within the next 3 business days** — further out kills momentum.
Send reminders at **24 hours and 1 hour** before, each with fresh value (a case
study, a tease, "we've already started prepping"). Frame as low-pressure
("15-minute strategy chat," not "sales call").

### Stage 8 — Closing to Payment
> *"Excited to get started! Once payment is set, we can kick off [specific next
> step]. Need any help with that?"*

Assumes the sale is already happening, names a concrete deliverable that starts
*only after* payment (creating urgency to pay), proactively offers to remove
payment friction.

## 5. The Trust Stack: Belief × Clarity × Safety

The Six Gates map the buyer's *journey over time*. The Trust Stack maps *why a
specific interaction — a DM, a call, a post — failed right now.* Use Six Gates
for "where is this lead," use the Trust Stack for "why did this exact
conversation die."

**Multiplicative** — one layer at zero, total trust is zero regardless of the
other two. Most failed sales conversations are creators fixing the wrong layer.

- **Belief** — do they believe *you specifically* can help *them specifically*?
  Broken by: generic positioning ("I help coaches scale"), overclaiming,
  performed confidence. Fixed by: specificity over scale, live thinking on their
  exact problem instead of declared expertise, coherence between energy and
  claims.
- **Clarity** — can they actually absorb what's being transmitted, at their
  bandwidth, right now? Broken by: dumping the full methodology in one message,
  switching formats (DM → email → video → PDF), being clever instead of plain.
  Fixed by: one idea per message, one clear next action (not three options),
  matching depth to where they are.
- **Safety** — does moving toward you feel somatically safe, independent of
  logic? Broken by: urgency language, hidden complexity, vague delivery ("we'll
  figure it out"), a mismatch between words and energy. Fixed by: transparency
  about process, explicit optionality ("you can pause anytime"), naming the risk
  out loud before they have to.

**Diagnostic table — use this literally on stalled leads:**

| What you saw | Layer that broke |
|---|---|
| "This looks amazing" then ghost | Safety — your energy spiked risk |
| They compliment but never buy | Belief — too generic, don't see themselves in it |
| Consume content endlessly, never reach out | Clarity — landing as information, not activation |
| Lots of questions mid-call | Belief + Clarity — don't feel specifically seen, or can't grasp the system |
| Verbal yes, then stalls after paying | Safety — nervous system distrusts the delivery process |
| "I need to think about it" | Usually Safety — hesitation = uncompressed fear, not real deliberation |
| Saves your post, never engages | Clarity — valuable but overloaded their bandwidth |
| Refers others, won't buy themselves | Safety — believes in you, fears personal exposure |

**For a Coach Authority System prospect going cold after a good first exchange**:
check Safety first, not more proof. Nine times out of ten with a first-time
website/AI buyer, it's not doubting competence — committing ₹25–35K to something
intangible feels risky in a way they can't articulate. Fix: name the risk out
loud ("this requires your voice and offers within a week — if you can't do that
this month, let's talk after") before they protect against it silently.

## 6. Trust Decay Physics

- **Value-stacking has a documented decay curve**: Bonus #1 "nice." Bonus #2
  "generous." Bonus #3 "seems like a lot." Bonus #4 "why are they convincing me
  this hard?" Bonus #5 "I need to leave." Each added bonus past a low threshold
  increases *suspicion*, not perceived value. **Rule: one clean offer, not a
  stacked one.**
- **CTA fatigue compounds the same way**: 1st CTA = invitation. 2nd = pressure
  noted. 3rd = pattern recognized. 4th = defenses up. 5th = trust withdrawn. 6th
  = blocked. Argues directly against multi-touch sequences that escalate
  urgency — each nudge reads as a *tell*.
- **Trust behaves like energy, not a switch**: conserved, accumulated, or leaked
  suddenly through a pressure spike. Why "great DM conversation → dead silence at
  the pitch" happens: the transition from low-pressure nurture to high-pressure
  ask is too fast a spike; the nervous system reads the spike itself as the
  threat. **Rule: the ask should feel like the same energy as the nurture, not a
  gear change.**
- **The buyer isn't ghosting, they're metabolizing**: silence, an unfinished DM,
  a sales page visited 17 times — gate-specific diagnostic data (Gate 4 spiked =
  risk; Gate 5 stalled = no bandwidth; Gate 6 wrestling = identity), not
  rejection. **Reframes "no response" from failure to information.**

## 7. Signal Lag discipline — don't panic-pivot

Trust compounds invisibly and non-linearly, then surfaces all at once (the DM
that says "I've been watching you for months"). **The single biggest
trust-destroying mistake**: changing the message/positioning/offer right when
flat engagement makes it look like nothing is working — forces every silent,
half-convinced observer to restart their trust calculation from zero.

**Practical rule**: if a positioning angle, content pillar, or offer framing
feels "flat" for a few weeks with no visible engagement, that is *not* sufficient
evidence to change it — flat metrics + silence is what compounding trust looks
like from the outside, right before it converts. Change signal only on positive
evidence it's wrong, not the absence of applause.

**The Signal-to-Leverage Converter** — four moves for when someone finally raises
their hand after a long silent period:
1. **Precision offer calibration** — don't pitch your newest framing; mirror back
   the exact concepts/phrases in their first message.
2. **Content asset rotation** — resurface old frameworks they've already
   absorbed rather than teaching something new in the close.
3. **Pre-sold application pathways** — reframe intake from evaluative ("what's
   your biggest challenge?") to confirmatory ("which of my frameworks have you
   already tried?").
4. **Referral echo loops** — people who convert after long silent observation
   are disproportionately likely to bring others from the same "silent
   cohort" — invite it explicitly once they convert.

## 8. Objection-to-gate phrase table

| What they say | Gate it signals | What it actually means |
|---|---|---|
| "I need to check with my partner" | Identity (G6) | Needs someone else to voice an identity fear they won't own directly |
| "Let me think about how this applies to my situation" | Fit (G3) | Understands the category, not their specific configuration — feels almost-fit, not fit |
| "This sounds amazing but I'm swamped right now" | Inertia (G5) | Real bandwidth math, not a brush-off — don't push, shrink the ask |
| "Let me see where things are in a few months" | Identity (G6) | The offer requires becoming someone they're not sure they want to be yet |
| "Can you send more info?" | Risk (G4) | Buying time/distance from a threat they can't name, not requesting data |
| "How long have you been doing this?" | Credibility (G1) | A safety probe, not genuine interest in your timeline |

The fix for each row is not more persuasion on that topic — it's addressing the
specific gate. "I need to check with my partner" isn't solved with a better
guarantee; it's solved by giving identity-safe language to bring back to the
partner.

## 9. The Zero-Pressure Close

The book refuses to give a script (scripts fail because trust runs on
transmitted energy, not words) — it reframes what the ask *is*:

- **Trust × Time = Inevitability.** More trust compounding before the ask means
  less energy the ask requires. Front-loaded effort (consistent signal over
  months) creates back-loaded ease; back-loaded effort (pushing hard at the
  pitch) creates front-loaded difficulty every time, because nothing compounds.
- **"The offer as resolution, not proposition."** In a well-built trust field,
  the offer's job isn't to create desire or overcome objections — it's to give
  an already-made decision somewhere to land. An offer that only explains
  logistics (what it is, who it's for, how it works, what happens next)
  converts pre-decided buyers better than one that tries to persuade — persuasive
  language itself signals you doubt they're already sold.
- **Integration Velocity** — how fast and frictionlessly someone moves from
  interest to action is a direct read of trust health, better than conversion
  rate alone. Resolution-ready signals: questions shift from evaluative ("what
  do you do?") to logistical ("how does this work / when do we start?"); their
  energy is calm rather than nervously excited; they start using your own
  language back to you unprompted. **Rule: don't make the explicit ask until you
  see at least one of these.**
- **Objection dissolution vs. handling** — a persistent objection after a
  well-run trust sequence is data that this isn't a fit, not a hurdle to script
  past ("better to discover incompatibility than force false fit").

**What this book explicitly does NOT give**: no word-for-word DM scripts, no
cold-outreach templates, no objection-rebuttal scripts — a deliberate stance. Its
actual contribution is diagnostic frameworks (Trust Stack, Signal Lag, gate-phrase
mapping) and a philosophy for *when* the ask should happen, not *what words* to
use.

## 10. Follow-up cadence

**Sell-By-Chat's stated fact**: 80% of sales happen after the 5th touchpoint —
most people quit before that. Cadence for a lead gone quiet:
- **Day 1**: follow up the day after first message/call.
- **Day 3**: a light check-in.
- **Day 7**: a "still here" nudge.
- **After day 7**: space follow-ups every couple of weeks rather than stopping
  entirely.

This is a *shorter-range, chat-specific* cadence, complementing (not replacing)
the ~26-touch Trust Equity model in §2 — use this for warm/qualifying
conversations already underway, the 26-touch model for cold relationship-building
before real engagement starts.

**Follow-up message patterns**:
- Personal-touch revival: *"Remember how we talked about [specific challenge]? I
  just helped a client with something similar and the results were bananas —
  want me to share what we did?"*
- Fresh-value revival: *"I just worked with someone in a similar spot and
  thought of you — want to hear how we made it work?"*
- Low-effort close-out: *"Want me to send the details?"*

**Practical tracking**: New Leads → Awaiting Reply → Replied → Qualifying → Call
Booked → Closed-Won/Lost, with last-message-direction, next-step, and
budget/timeline/key-problem notes per conversation.

## 11. Task recipe: DM review / drafting a DM

1. Classify the DM type — cold, intent-based, or inbound (§1) — before doing
   anything else. Each has a different job (open Gate 1, open Gate 2, move fast
   through Gate 3).
2. If cold: check it against the 7-part/3-layer template and the "makes sense to
   no one else" test.
3. If intent-based: confirm it references the *specific* trigger (which post,
   which comment), not a generic "saw you engaged."
4. If inbound: check speed-to-lead and whether it lets them vent before
   qualifying.
5. If it's a **reply/qualifying** message: check it against the LVQ rhythm and
   the A–B gap-widening method (§4, Stage 2), and confirm only one question is
   being asked.
6. If it's a **stalled conversation**: run the Trust Stack diagnostic table (§5)
   before rewriting anything — the fix depends on which layer broke, not on
   adding more proof by default.
7. If it's an **objection response**: map it to a gate using §8, and check for
   Acknowledge → Ask for context → Reassure, with zero "buts."
8. If it's a **close/booking message**: check against Stage 6–8 (§4) — no
   calendar link, times within 3 business days, an assumptive payment close.
9. Across all of the above: check for Trust Decay Physics violations (§6) —
   stacked bonuses, CTA fatigue, urgency spikes that don't match the
   conversation's prior energy.
