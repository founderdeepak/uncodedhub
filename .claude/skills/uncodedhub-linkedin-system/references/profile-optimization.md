## 2. Headline (≤220 characters, truncates at ~60 on mobile)

**Never start with "I help X do Y."** This is named as the single most generic,
most competed-on structure on the platform — it describes a service, not a world.

**Real examples from the source, all under 70 characters, none use "I help":**
- Bec (agricultural strategy): *"Commercial Strategy for Ag-Facing Businesses |
  Trusted by Producers"* — uses the tribe's own self-name ("producers," not
  "farmers"), plus a trust claim instead of a service claim.
- Bill Krell (recruiter): *"Engineers are underpaid (except for mine)"* — names
  the tribe's shared enemy/fence in six words, then claims the position.
- Chester (sleep coach): *"Business owners putting in the hours but operating at
  60%"* — describes the tribe's *felt experience*, no service category named at
  all.

**Two usable templates:**
- **[Tribal identity/language] | [what they get from you]**
- **[Tribal pain point] | [your position]**
- Or a single felt-experience sentence (Chester's pattern).

Once a sub-niche is confirmed (`niche-and-tribe.md`), pull the actual tribal
vocabulary from the 5-Word Test / dossier language room — never generic marketing
words.

## 3. About section / DM opener

Longer form than the headline — can go deeper.

**Structure**: one sentence, world-first, no "I help," stacking 2–3 tribe layers.
Example given: *"Rural ag operators in succession planning who can't figure out
how to hand over something that runs on knowledge nobody wrote down"* (Chosen +
Circumstantial + Psychic layer, one sentence).

Bec's About opens with a Level-5 lived-experience line: *"Most ag-facing
businesses don't lose deals because of capability — they lose them because
producers don't trust them yet"* — then her actual biography (grew up on the
farm, both sides of the fence).

**Hit Carrier Trust directly in the copy** (full breakdown in
`content-and-virality.md` §1):
- Real motive stated plainly, even bluntly.
- One honest "real-life mess" beat — imperfection, not a highlight reel.
- Visible skin-in-the-game (real client results, real effort/time committed).
- A genuine point of view worth disagreeing with — "pick a side" without going
  political/religious.

## 4. Banner

Explicitly called **"a tribe declaration, not a branding decision."** A generic
city-skyline/gradient banner wastes the single most pre-verbal piece of real
estate on the profile.

Example: Bec's banner is a photograph of a cotton farm with plain text, no CTA —
credited as doing *more* pre-verbal recognition work than her headline.

**Direct action item**: whatever sub-niche is picked, the banner should visually
signal that specific world (an image tribe members recognize before reading
anything), not a generic "professional services" look. (No visual kit loaded yet
— see §1. Describe the *concept* the banner should communicate; don't produce a
mockup.)

## 5. Featured section

Used as the **evergreen lead-magnet slot** — captures traffic between viral
posts and compounds every future post's effect (see
`lead-magnets-and-email.md` §5 for the full comment→DM→delivery mechanic this
supports). Place a preview of the current lead magnet here with a clear "Get the
free guide" CTA, backed by a dedicated squeeze page if the website has one.

## 6. Content-depth self-audit (Five Levels, applied to the profile/posts)

Pull the last 5 posts and score each 1–5 on the same scale used for content
review (`content-and-virality.md` §7 has the full pattern list; the Five Levels
themselves live here since they're a profile/content-audit diagnostic first):

1. **Label** — "I help agricultural businesses grow." Zero sensory detail,
   describes millions of people.
2. **Stacked Labels** — adds modifiers but still describes *what* they are, not
   what their world *feels* like. Where most niching worksheets/ICP docs stop.
3. **World Description** — sounds informed (names real conditions) but could be
   written after an afternoon of desk research. Smart, not specific.
4. **Tribal Content (the threshold)** — names actual Kitchen Counter items
   (specific policy/brand/event happening *right now* — see
   `niche-and-tribe.md` §5). Formula: Kitchen Counter item + tribal
   interpretation + lived experience = Level 4 minimum. Recognition starts
   firing here.
5. **Director's Cut** — speaks *as* the tribe, from decades of lived membership,
   with earned anger/opinion.

**Diagnostic**: check the comments on each post specifically. Level 4+ gets
insider-language comments that extend the conversation in the tribe's own
vocabulary; Level 1–3 gets generic praise ("great post!" + emoji).

## 7. Task recipe: running a profile audit

1. Confirm the sub-niche is defined first (`niche-and-tribe.md`) — a profile audit
   before the tribe is chosen can only fix generic problems, not positioning ones.
2. **Headline**: rewrite using §2's templates and the confirmed tribe's actual
   5-Word-Test vocabulary. Flag if it still reads as "I help X do Y."
3. **About**: check against the Carrier Trust checklist in §3 line by line — real
   motive, one mess beat, skin-in-the-game, a real point of view.
4. **Banner**: describe what it should visually signal for this tribe (§4); note
   the visual kit gap plainly rather than inventing a design.
5. **Featured**: confirm a current lead magnet preview lives here, tied to the
   funnel in `lead-magnets-and-email.md` §5.
6. **Content-depth check**: score the last 5 posts 1–5 (§6) and read the comments
   — insider language or generic praise.
7. **Final gate test**: would Gates 1–2 ("Is this real?" / "Is this for me?" — full
   definitions in `content-and-virality.md` §2) open for the specific sub-niche
   tribe within 3 seconds of landing on the profile? If not, say which element is
   still failing the test and fix that one first.
