### Email 3 — Day 5: Pain 1 — The trust gap (PAS)

**Subject:** the prospect who almost said yes

**Problem:** Somewhere in the last few months, someone found your business, looked genuinely interested — and then just... disappeared. No complaint, no "we went with someone else" email. Just gone.

**Agitate:** That's the part that should actually worry you. It's not that you lost a sale — it's that you have no idea how many times that's already happened, because a lost prospect never tells you why. It just shows up later as a quieter month, and you blame the season, or the ads, or bad luck.

**Solution:** In our own client work, the fix is never "make it look nicer." It's mapping exactly what a skeptical stranger is silently thinking in the first 20 seconds — the objections they won't say out loud, the proof they're scanning for before they'll trust you with money. Most sites answer none of that. The ones that convert answer almost all of it before the prospect ever picks up the phone.

If your audit score was under 14, this is almost certainly where you're leaking.

**P.S.** — If you want a second pair of eyes on your specific score, reply "AUDIT CALL" and I'll walk through it with you directly, free, 20 minutes.

### Email 4 — Day 9: Pain 2 — The personal-selling bottleneck (PAS)

**Subject:** the ceiling nobody talks about

**Problem:** Right now, if you disappeared for two weeks, would your business still close new clients?

For most of the owners I talk to, the honest answer is no — because they're still the one doing the convincing. Every call, every DM, every in-person walk-through, it's them, personally, re-explaining why they're worth it.

**Agitate:** That's not a workload problem. It's a growth ceiling. The business can only grow as fast as one person can personally sell — and that person is also supposed to be doing the actual work they're good at.

**Solution:** A website's real job isn't decoration — it's doing the first half of that convincing before the call even happens, so the owner's job becomes choosing which leads to take, not persuading every single one from scratch. That's the entire difference between a website and what we actually build.

**P.S.** — Curious where you'd put yourself on this: are you still the only person in your business who can close a sale? Hit reply, I read these.

### Email 5 — Day 12: Pain 3 — Invisibility in search (PAS)

**Subject:** invisible and available aren't the same thing

**Problem:** Somewhere near you, right now, someone is actively searching for exactly what you offer.

**Agitate:** If your Google Business Profile is stale, your site isn't built for local search, and there's no groundwork helping AI-driven search surfaces (the tools more people now search through instead of a plain Google box) find and describe your business accurately — you're not losing to a better competitor. You're losing to someone who was simply easier to find at the exact moment demand existed.

Being great and being invisible produce the same revenue: none, from the people who never found you.

**Solution:** This is the third leg of what we actually build — local SEO groundwork and AI-visibility basics alongside the site itself, not a separate expensive retainer bolted on after.

**P.S.** — Quick gut check: when's the last time you actually searched for your own business the way a stranger would?

## Ongoing Weekly Cadence (post Day-14, indefinite)

Three emails per week, fixed rotation. Cut to one per week if three isn't sustainable — consistency beats volume.

- **Story + Lesson** (e.g. Monday) — a real, personal anecdote pivoting into a lesson tied to the trust-gap/selling-bottleneck/visibility themes. Soft CTA only.
- **Case Study / Social Proof** (e.g. Wednesday) — **only once real, honest client results exist.** Until then, replace with a "process spotlight" (e.g. walking through one real element of the build process, like the performance-standard checks) rather than fabricating a case study — per this project's honesty rule, no invented client stories.
- **Free Resource / Extra Value** (e.g. Friday) — a new mini-resource (a Loom teardown, a checklist, a template) that invites a reply and opens a 1:1 thread.

---

## Monthly Offer Campaign — Whisper → Tease → Shout

One deliberate sales push per month, escalating over four weeks. Rotate which niche/avatar and which of the three pains gets featured each month (interior designers this month, coaches next, etc.) so the same list gets fresh, relevant framing over time.

- **Weeks 1–3 (Whisper):** soft CTAs folded into the regular weekly cadence emails — no dedicated "sales" email yet.
- **Week 4, first email (Tease):** short, plain-text "something's coming" note, pure anticipation, no offer detail yet.
- **Week 4, last email (Shout):** direct, friendly invite — "We're taking on [N] new builds this month for [niche/pain focus] — want in?" Framed as genuinely limited (real capacity constraint: two-person, senior-only delivery), never fabricated urgency.

---

## AIDA Follow-Up (for replies that stall post-offer)

When someone replies to the Day 14 invite or a monthly Shout but doesn't book:

1. Send a short, specific next step — 2 concrete time slots or a one-line doc addressing their specific hesitation.
2. If they go quiet, run up to **4 follow-ups over 1–2 weeks**, each casual and specific (reference something real from their earlier reply), never templated-sounding.
3. Final follow-up gives a graceful, low-pressure exit: "No worries either way — I'll check back in a few months. The audit's still yours regardless."

## Copy rules carried over from the project's honesty rule

- Every verifiable claim (guarantee terms, performance standards, delivery model) must stay true to what the live site actually offers.
- Replace bracketed placeholders ([Deepak], [Name], [link]) before sending; this file is the reusable template, not the sent copy.
